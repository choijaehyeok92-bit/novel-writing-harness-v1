# World Bible Overrides — Chapters 116–117

> These entries add enacted geopolitical and institutional facts from chapters 116–117. They do not alter unrelated earlier world-bible material.

## Two-Romes conflict state

- The conflict is no longer merely preparatory. From chapter 116 onward, a coordinated proxy conflict is operating across Hungary, Serbia, Anatolia, Cilicia, the Adriatic and western Europe.
- Henry's coalition does not function as a centralized modern alliance. Each participant pursues a separate dynastic, territorial, religious, commercial or survival interest.
- Alexios's network likewise functions through layered legal relations: direct vassals, dependent rulers, allied kings, local rival houses, Roman troops, intelligence, finance and naval power.

## Hungary

- Andrew's claim is sustained by German support and Venetian finance/material.
- By chapter 117 the effective political-military division is around the Drava crossings:
  - Andrew: much of Croatia, Dalmatia, Slavonia.
  - Emeric: Hungarian heartland and central/northern Pannonia.
- Alexios Gidos has now commanded Roman troops in direct support of Emeric.

## Serbia

- Serbia remains a direct Roman vassal under Stefan Nemanjić.
- Vukan's western/coastal rebellion is supported through Henry–Andrew–Venetian channels.
- Kaloyan has now commanded a Roman tagma in direct support of Stefan.
- The hardened internal front is described through upper Lim, west of Ras, the Ibar valley and Zvečan.

## Anatolia / dependent Seljuk polity

- Kaykhusraw remains the Roman-backed sultan.
- Qutb al-Din Malikshah survives in this timeline and has emerged as an anti-Roman rival based from Sivas.
- Caesarea is a defended key city with Roman military presence and logistical support.
- A Roman-vassal Danishmendid polity exists around Niksar–Tokat–Amasya and attacks Malikshah's rear.
- Manuscript spelling for the associated house/group is `즐눈계`; preserve it unless the author later normalizes it.

## Cilician Armenia

- Levon has entered the anti-Roman alignment but is constrained internally.
- Lampron Hetumids actively oppose him with Roman encouragement.
- Lampron is established as sufficiently strong to pin a substantial part of Levon's field capacity even though the Hetumids cannot destroy the Rubenid regime.

## Adriatic

- Roman naval forces now conduct interdiction operations against Venetian shipping supplying anti-Roman rebels.
- Venice can respond strongly enough that Roman admirals avoid unnecessary battle in waters favorable to Venice.
- This creates a security dilemma that shifts Venetian domestic politics toward Dandolo without yet establishing total Venetian unity or general naval war.

## France / Angevin realm

- Richard I deliberately challenges the feudal relationship between his kingship and Philip II's suzerainty over continental fiefs.
- His jurists formulate an argument from royal anointing and crowned status.
- Philip rejects this and frames Richard as a rebellious continental vassal.
- Henry supports Philip through finance, recruitment access, military specialists and limited gunpowder technology.

## Finance

- Roman finance remains stable through the initial chapter-116 Venetian pressure because Koinē Trapeza purchases support `황은채`.
- By the end of chapter 117, Venice prepares a larger assault aimed directly at Roman `trapezai`, credit and financial confidence.
- This new attack is **not yet resolved**.
