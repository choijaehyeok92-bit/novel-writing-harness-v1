# Arc 5 Update — Chapters 116–117

## Arc 5 — After Barbarossa / Western Succession Rivalry → Two-Romes Proxy War

### New core question

Can Henry break Alexios's system by forcing simultaneous crises across the imperial periphery before the two emperors meet in direct war?

### Locked development

- Chapter 116 converts the post-Sicily rivalry into an active **multi-theater proxy war from 1196 onward**.
- Henry VI's strategic method is coalition construction:
  - Andrew destabilizes Hungary,
  - Vukan destabilizes Serbia,
  - Malikshah destabilizes the Roman-dependent Seljuk settlement,
  - Levon pressures the Cilician/Antiochene settlement,
  - Philip II checks Richard's Angevin power,
  - Venice attacks both maritime access and Roman finance.
- Alexios's answer is not immediate mass mobilization. He relies on:
  - legitimate local rulers,
  - vassal states,
  - intelligence,
  - limited elite Roman formations,
  - advisers,
  - supply systems,
  - controlled gunpowder diffusion,
  - naval interdiction,
  - financial stabilization,
  - local rival houses such as the Lampron Hetumids.
- Chapter 117 proves that this distributed response works well enough to create **stalemate rather than collapse**.

### Front results by chapter 117

- Hungary: Andrew's advance stops after the Balaton counterstroke.
- Serbia: Vukan's advance stops after Raška; Zvečan anchors the hardened front.
- Anatolia: Malikshah cannot take Caesarea and is harassed in his rear.
- Cilicia: Levon is pinned by Lampron.
- Adriatic: Rome and Venice confront one another but avoid general naval battle.
- Western Europe: Richard–Philip conflict opens as a fresh counter-front against Henry's wider coalition.
- Finance: Dandolo prepares to turn Venetian commercial-financial capability into the next offensive instrument.

### Arc-function change

Before ch116, the Great War was primarily a looming future escalation.
After ch117, the **Two-Romes war has already begun in proxy, financial, dynastic and legal forms**, even though the principal emperors have not yet met in the decisive direct campaign.

### Current dramatic pressure

- Can Dandolo's larger second financial attack succeed where earlier bond pressure failed?
- Can Henry keep subsidizing Andrew, Vukan, Philip and eastern proxies without exhausting imperial political capital?
- Can Roman allies remain loyal under prolonged stalemate?
- Does Richard's break with Philip relieve pressure on Alexios or merely create another uncontrollable war?
- When does Henry conclude that proxies cannot break the Roman system and commit to direct invasion?
