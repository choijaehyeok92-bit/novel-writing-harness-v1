# Arc 5 Update — Chapter 121

## Roman counterattack → Venetian constitutional crisis

Chapter 121 moves the Saint Mark Panic aftermath from interstate financial retaliation into an internal Venetian constitutional struggle.

## Roman position

- The Roman government now possesses documentary evidence tying Venetian merchant houses to the coordinated Saint Mark Day attack: cipher traffic, a decoded text, and a decryption key/table.
- The Venetian bailo protests but cannot overturn the Roman evidentiary case.
- Rome deliberately does not repeat the 1171 mass arrest model; the threat remains available but unused.
- The Roman message is deterrent rather than immediate declaration of war: Venice is warned that escalation risks total loss.

## Dandolo after the failed panic

Dandolo does not interpret the failure as proof that confrontation was mistaken.

He interprets it as proof that:
- Rome's institutions are already dangerously resilient;
- time is running out;
- Venice must act before the relative gap becomes irreversible.

His alliance with Henry VI is explicitly instrumental:
- Dandolo distrusts both Roman emperors;
- he prefers the western emperor as the temporarily more dependent partner because Henry lacks an autonomous maritime system in the current timeline.

This completes Dandolo's shift from strategic rivalry to self-identification with the survival of Venice:
**he increasingly treats opposition to his strategy as opposition to the republic itself.**

## Ziani faction

Pietro Ziani, Domenico Sanudo, Pietro Michiel and Ottaviano Querini organize an elite opposition coalition.

Their planned route is constitutional rather than revolutionary:
- turn the Minor Council against Dandolo;
- deny Great Council support for further escalation and war spending;
- demand negotiations with Rome;
- use the Saint Mark operation and the ducal oath as grounds for forcing resignation.

The opposition believes Dandolo can be politically neutralized without bloodshed.

## Dandolo's answer

Before the opposition can move, armed German-accented men seize the Ziani meeting.

This is not yet presented as an official mobilization of Venetian state forces.
The men:
- act coercively outside the councils,
- accuse the opposition of Eastern Roman collusion,
- promise forced "retirement" rather than immediate execution.

The move constitutes a **constitutional rupture / counter-coup**:
Dandolo preserves his position by bypassing the institutions designed to constrain the doge.

## Thematic mirror

The chapter deliberately mirrors chapters 119–120.

- Alexios, faced with crisis, lets the Syllogos review budgets and keeps administration/audit/inquiry roles institutionally separated.
- Dandolo, faced with a council revolt, turns to off-book force.

Both men claim to act for their state.
Their methods now diverge sharply.

- The fate of Pietro Ziani and the other detained elites.

- Whether German-accented mercenaries can remain deniable.
