# Arc 5 Update — Chapter 118

## Phase shift: proxy war → financial war

Chapter 118 marks a major escalation in the Two-Romes conflict.

Henry VI's coalition has failed to produce rapid Roman collapse on the military proxy fronts. Venice therefore attacks the **financial architecture** that made Alexios's imperial recovery possible.

## Saint Mark Panic arc function

The crisis tests whether Alexios's reforms created only prosperity or also a state capable of surviving the second-order risks created by that prosperity.

The panic is not framed as a simple foreign sabotage story:
- Venetian action is the trigger.
- Roman financial innovation created the structural vulnerability.
- Leon Chrysocheir's aggressive model concentrates systemic risk.
- Koini Trapeza's existence creates a moral-hazard expectation.
- Rumor and rational self-protection turn the attack into a self-sustaining run.

## Dandolo's strengthened antagonist role

- Dandolo understands Roman institutions rather than merely hating them.
- He deliberately weaponizes market confidence, payment timing, bond prices and the rescue expectations created by Koini Trapeza.
- He accepts that the attack may cost Venice its remaining Roman privileges because he needs a fast political result before western subsidy burdens and internal opposition worsen.

## New institutional test

The chapter ends by naming three institutions:
- Koini Trapeza,
- Synkletos,
- Syllogos.

Their detailed crisis response remains unresolved and is the next arc beat.

## Current dramatic questions

- Does the state save Chrysocheir, the payment system, depositors, or some combination?
- Can Koini Trapeza halt a run without validating Leon's moral hazard?
- Will the Synkletos and Syllogos authorize emergency fiscal support and on what conditions?
- Does the financial defense prove Alexios's system stronger, or expose a constitutional bottleneck during wartime?
- What price will Venice pay if the attack fails?
