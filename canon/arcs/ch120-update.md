# Arc 5 Update — Chapter 120

## Saint Mark Panic: institutional survival → constitutional gain → counterattack

Chapter 120 resolves the parliamentary test left open at the end of chapter 119 and converts the failed Venetian attempt at rapid financial coercion into a broader Roman counteroffensive.

## Political / constitutional payoff

- The emergency Syllogos sitting establishes that the body has grown from its original 166-seat apportionment to **216 seats** after population growth and recovery/integration of Bucellarian and Anatolikon territories.
- **165 members** attend the emergency sitting.
- The 1196-majority Kyanophoroi are temporarily outnumbered in the room because their constituency and membership are more provincial/rural and many members have not yet arrived.
- Asen supports anti-Venetian retaliation but refuses a simple banker bailout.
- His challenge converts the crisis into a constitutional bargaining moment.
- Alexios rejects direct legislative participation in treasury execution but grants a durable **pre-finalization Syllogos budget-review and recommendation practice**.
- The emperor remains legally unbound, making this a self-imposed constitutional convention rather than modern parliamentary sovereignty.
- The 165 members present unanimously support the supplementary budget; the vote exceeds two-thirds of the full house and goes directly to the emperor.

## Institutional division of labor — strengthened

The crisis now leaves a clearer practical separation:
- **Emperor/cabinet:** execution and emergency policy.
- **Syllogos:** budget review, recommendation and political fiscal legitimacy.
- **Logisterion:** professional audit/financial examination.
- **Synkletos:** public inquiry/hearing and political accountability.

The crisis therefore strengthens institutions rather than merely proving they can survive.

## Saint Mark Panic outcome

- The panic is substantially stabilized within about one month.
- Leon/failed trapezitai are not treated as equivalent to depositors or the payment system.
- Public intervention remains justified as protection of deposits, payment functions and systemic stability, not reward for reckless owners.

## Roman counterattack against Venice

Alexios answers Dandolo's financial attack with a layered economic-war package:
- escrow of Venetian receivables pending compensation,
- preservation of legal ownership of escrowed funds,
- temporary suspension of Venetian commercial privileges,
- additional collateral and controlled clearing for Venetian trade,
- exclusion of Venetian institutions from Koini Trapeza emergency funding,
- preservation of `황은채` validity while Venetian payments are placed in escrow,
- conditional protection of neutral Venetian houses that open their books and swear commercial neutrality,
- commercial substitution through Genoa and Pisa,
- Adriatic diplomatic/covert pressure through Emeric, Dalmatian cities, Ancona and Zadar.

## Dandolo's strategic result — updated

Dandolo's financial attack remains a genuine tactical success because it:
- causes a major Roman panic,
- consumes fiscal capacity,
- forces emergency financial interventions,
- creates political controversy over private risk and public money.

But its immediate coercive objective fails by chapter 120:
- Roman payments and sovereign credit survive,
- the supplementary budget passes,
- the panic stabilizes,
- Roman institutions gain new political legitimacy and powers,
- Venice becomes the target of organized financial and commercial retaliation.

The final strategic outcome remains open because Venetian domestic reaction, compensation negotiations and the broader Two-Romes war have not yet resolved.

## Next immediate pressures

- Dandolo's response to the Roman escrow/privilege regime.
- Pietro Ziani and the anti-war Venetian faction's reaction to the economic cost.
- Whether neutral Venetian merchant houses break political solidarity with the doge.
- What compensation, if any, Venice is willing to negotiate.
- How much market share Genoa and Pisa can gain without becoming replacement monopolists.
- Whether Ancona, Zadar and other Dalmatian actors accept Roman commercial/political support.
- Whether the new Syllogos budget-review convention survives a future emperor less willing to accept scrutiny.
