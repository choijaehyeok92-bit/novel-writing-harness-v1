## From Bouvines shock to direct war

Chapter 124 supplies the political bridge behind Henry's chapter-123 decision to attack Eastern Rome.

1. Richard's victorious position threatens Henry's western rear through Welf and other hostile networks.
2. Conrad negotiates hereditary/succession assurances and non-support for Edward's opponents in exchange for reciprocal nonaggression and non-intervention.
3. The secret pact removes Richard's active military pressure but does not make him an anti-Roman ally or end his friendship with Alexios.
4. Henry's proxy system continues to deteriorate: Qutb al-Din is murdered by his supporters; Levon remains tied down by the Hetumids; Andrew and Vukan struggle against Roman-supported opponents.
5. Pro-Dandolo merchant houses have financed a substantial share of Andrew/Vukan support against promised privileges and war revenues. Increasingly tied-up money threatens Dandolo's regime and Henry's coalition.
6. Henry buys domestic cooperation through legal/property concessions and Frederick VI's rehabilitation, obtaining agreed troops, transport and domestic peace pledges.
7. Henry then orders his ministeriales forward without a prior ultimatum, invoking Sicilian claims through Constance and Andrew/Vukan's requests.

## The cost of stabilization

Henry wins time and troops by accepting limits on the reform program he wants to complete. His calculation is that conquest and the prestige of both Romes will enable him to recover the concessions: `따서 갚으면 된다.`

The princely compact is not unconditional judicial restraint: the source retains an urgent-imperial-intervention exception. Preserve that residual ambiguity instead of silently improving the bargain.

Frederick is a loyal released brother who accepts participation, not a newly invented rebel or a regent appointed in Henry's absence.

## Richard and Alexios

Richard prioritizes Edward's secure inheritance. Ana sees betrayal; Alexios regards Richard as having already accomplished more than the minimum strategic task by defeating Philip and removing one hostile axis.

Alexios's confidence rests on the time gained for Roman preparation and the state he has built. He summons the existing Sekreton tou Stratopedou. Do not interpret this as proof he dictated Richard's choices or already knows every detail of Henry's operation.
