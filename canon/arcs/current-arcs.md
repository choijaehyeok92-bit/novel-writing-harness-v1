# Arc Canon Through Chapter 110

## Arc 1 — Survival, Regency, Andronikos (ch1–26) — COMPLETED

### Core question
Can a child emperor who knows his historical murder survive without becoming another palace tyrant?

### Locked beats
- 1180-08-01 possession/transfer.
- Manuel I is persuaded to divide and constrain regency power rather than concentrate it.
- Alexios builds parallel anchors in Church, army, navy, bureaucracy and information.
- Venetian/Latin question becomes a deliberate attempt to prevent the historical anti-Latin spiral.
- Court enemies and Andronikos exploit real grievances.
- Karax battle breaks Andronikos militarily.
- Hippodrome trial lets Andronikos articulate true systemic problems; Alexios concedes them and steals his reform mantle.
- Andronikos executed; collaborators punished; Alexios recognizes that victory creates a debt to actually reform the problems he admitted.

### Character result
Alexios moves from "survive three years" to "become the ruler capable of changing the system."

## Arc 2 — Reform State and Norman War (ch27–48) — COMPLETED, DATE CLEANUP OPEN

### Core question
Can Alexios turn future knowledge into institutions quickly enough to survive the 1185 strategic crisis?

### Locked beats
- tax/land/accounting reform and new advisory talent.
- paper, arithmetic/bookkeeping, technical workshop, printing.
- old elite resistance shifts from court intrigue to legal/political organization.
- merit recruitment, army command restructuring, fortress redesign.
- Angelos/Palaiologos conspiracy and Philadelphia disturbance contained.
- Norman invasion defeated decisively around Dyrrachium; Alexios wins overwhelming legitimacy.
- Sicily forced into reparations/constraints; Serbia resubordinated.
- postwar legitimacy spent on 100-seat Synkletos, 166-seat Syllogos architecture, nine-logothesion cabinet, high-court reform, university expansion.
- Cheirobolon reaches operational reliability by 1187-08-04.

### Open continuity issue
The manuscript's explicit years around ch26–40 contradict chapter order. Keep event order but do not invent a corrected calendar until author decision.

## Arc 3 — Two Romes and the Great Crusade (ch49–93) — COMPLETED

### Core question
Can Alexios use the Jerusalem crisis to reconcile enough of East/West to recover Anatolia and the Holy City without surrendering Roman identity?

### Locked beats
- Jerusalem falls; Eastern patriarchal crusade initiative precedes/pressures Western response.
- Syllogos first election and sitting occur under war-tax/Latin anxiety; early parties emerge.
- Diplomatic rapprochement with papacy and Frederick Barbarossa.
- Frederick crosses Roman lands peacefully and survives; Alexios and Frederick formalize a limited "Two Romes" partnership.
- Gunpowder combined formations enter field use.
- Philomelion/Iconium break Rum; Kaykhusraw becomes dependent ruler; direct Roman Anatolian recovery expands.
- Antioch/Cilicia brought into stronger Roman suzerainty arrangements.
- Acre/Damietta/Judean operations break Saladin's strategic position.
- Jerusalem restored by negotiated settlement; massacre prevented; Muslim worship/residence protected under a medieval legal hierarchy.
- Conrad I becomes king; rival western claims are managed.
- Coalition returns with True Cross relics and enormous legitimacy.
- Anna proves herself as long-duration regent.
- Friendship with Frederick transitions into rivalry as western empire learns gunpowder and prepares its own consolidation.

### Character result
Alexios becomes more than a survivor/reformer: he is now a sacral-military "recoverer" whose authority can support constitutional/legal innovations that would previously have been impossible.

## Arc 4 — Succession, Finance, Roman Identity, Integration (ch94–108) — COMPLETED

### Core question
Can a state built by one extraordinary emperor survive dynastic succession, financial complexity, religious pluralism and conquest-integration?

### Locked beats
- 1192 succession law codifies designation, order of succession, bicameral ratification, church coronation, army/citizen proclamation, regency, anti-usurpation and anti-mutilation rules.
- Child Manuel becomes co-emperor under the new legitimacy framework.
- Old guard retires/dies; generational change begins.
- Venice/Dandolo probes the new Roman financial system through state-debt pressure; Roman bond-market response holds.
- Logisterion is separated from Ophthalmos to prevent over-concentration of coercive/audit power.
- Spinning-wheel diffusion and Yusuf's promotion force the regime to define a political Roman identity broader than Orthodoxy.
- Four-field agriculture and pasture registration create both prosperity and nomad displacement.
- Alexios deliberately uses the crisis to separate tribes willing to register/contract from those willing to fight; cooperating communities receive protection and economic routes, resistant ones are destroyed.
- Imperial equestrian games convert Anatolian/Turkic martial skill into a shared public spectacle.
- Ch107: Anatolikon meros defeats Laskaris meros 4–3 and wins the Diskos tis Tikis.
- Ch108: the post-match imperial award ceremony establishes three-tier podium and gold/silver/bronze medal presentation as a new imperial sporting custom; Alexios explicitly frames the mixed Greek/Turkic Anatolikon victory as an image of political Roman identity.
- Ch108: the individual **Basilike Kelitika** culminates the festival. Muslim Turkic contract cavalryman **Kutlu Arslan**, serving with the Tourkopouloi, wins the **Mega Kypellon** on his black horse **Karagöz**. The Constantinopolitan crowd moves from chanting for the horse to chanting Arslan's name, showing genuine cultural incorporation without resolving deeper integration backlash.
- Ch108 also establishes that the imperial household has recently adopted the **double-headed eagle** as an imperial symbol and uses it on the Mega Kypellon.

### Character result
Alexios demonstrates that legal integration can be reinforced by shared popular culture, but the regime has not solved the deeper religious, ethnic, regional and class conflicts created by expansion.

## Arc 5 — After Barbarossa / Western Succession Rivalry (ch109–CURRENT) — CURRENT

### Core question
With Frederick Barbarossa dead, can the limited Two-Romes accommodation survive a colder Hohenstaufen successor while both empires compete for Roman legitimacy, Sicily, technology and Mediterranean power?

### Locked beats
- Ch109 opens with the second-order effects of the imperial sports festival: fandom, sponsorship interest, horse-breeding incentives, more Turkic submissions, merchandising and a mature print ecosystem.
- Constantinople now has official gazettes, aristocratic/private bulletins, party publications, and cheap rumor sheets known as **Kitrina Fyla / 키트리나 필라**. Alexios deliberately permits criticism and uses print alongside Ophthalmos reporting as a public-opinion instrument.
- Alexios develops the concept of **Ta Basilika Olympia**, a Christianized imperial multi-sport festival centered initially on Constantinople. This remains a **plan**, not an enacted institution.
- News arrives that Frederick Barbarossa died several weeks before the founding-day festival. Alexios orders Konstantinos Mesopotamites west with mourning honors and a strategic intelligence mandate concerning Henry VI.
- Ch109 retrospectively locks Frederick's post-crusade western consolidation: Reichsgut review, annual estate/regalia reporting, ministerial service, strengthened Landfrieden, city/minor-noble counterweights, guarded imperial gunpowder work, prevention of a renewed Henry-the-Lion-scale Saxon/Bavarian bloc, and a compact strengthening Hohenstaufen priority in election of a qualified legitimate eldest heir as King of the Romans while strengthening princely hereditary guarantees.
- Frederick's centralization is explicitly not a Byzantine-style unitary state. Its strength depends heavily on his sacral-military prestige as a recoverer of Jerusalem and therefore contains a succession problem.
- On his deathbed at Nuremberg Frederick directs Henry to secure his Sicilian rights, viewing Sicily as a separate dynastic resource capable of strengthening Hohenstaufen power without relying entirely on imperial princes. He dies with the dynastic formula: **"Rome to the Hohenstaufen."**
- Ch110 establishes Frederick's burial at **Speyer Cathedral** beside Beatrice. Alexios's mourning mission later places a silver-plated perpetual lamp at the tomb with a bilingual dedication recognizing Frederick as the western Imperator and Jerusalem comrade.
- **Frederick VI, Duke of Swabia, survives** his historical death and is established as a crusade hero, personally loyal to Henry and without overt throne ambition.
- Major princes visibly show Frederick VI unusual warmth, including Adolf of Altena, Hermann I of Thuringia, Henry I of Brabant, Henry III of Limburg and Albert I of Meissen. The manuscript frames this as a political signal against Henry's centralizing line, making Frederick an objective focal point whether he wants the role or not.
- Alexios predicts Henry will eventually confine Frederick rather than kill him, because execution would create a martyr and opposition banner. This is **prediction only; no confinement has yet happened on-page**.
- Mesopotamites deliberately does not recruit or contact Frederick as a Byzantine proxy. Alexios judges premature intervention would expose Byzantine intent and could provoke Henry into eliminating his brother.
- The western mission's immediate objective is to determine the scope of Henry's ambition: a tolerable strategic competitor seeking Sicily and balance, or a ruler seeking to make the western empire the sole Rome.
- Ch110 ends at the opening of the first formal audience. Henry is still **Romanorum Rex**, not yet Imperator; Mesopotamites addresses him by that precise title in public, immediately testing the transferability of Frederick's imperial recognition and Henry's Roman pretensions.

### Current character positions
- **Alexios II:** mourns Frederick genuinely but treats Henry as a likely strategic rival; refuses premature covert use of Frederick VI.
- **Henry VI:** inherits the machinery and resentments of Frederick's reforms; is presented as young, disciplined, cold, intelligent and politically dangerous. Imperial coronation has not yet occurred in this timeline.
- **Frederick VI of Swabia:** loyal younger brother and crusade hero whose personal lack of ambition does not reduce his objective value to discontented princes.
- **Konstantinos Mesopotamites:** envoy, intelligence assessor and diplomatic duelist; begins the Henry relationship by withholding the imperial title without violating formal accuracy.

### Active future pressures already seeded
- Henry's response to the `Romanorum Rex` titulature challenge and the future terms of recognition between the Two Romes.
- Henry's Sicilian claim after Frederick's death and Alexios's balance-of-power response.
- Whether princely resentment around Frederick VI becomes mediation, confinement, open coalition or civil conflict.
- Dandolo + Henry / Western imperial coordination against Roman commercial expansion.
- Papal leverage over Henry's imperial coronation and fear of a Hohenstaufen Germany–Sicily combination.
- Ayyubid post-Saladin succession.
- Balsamon/Church anxiety over secular political Roman identity and the planned Christianized Olympia.
- Whether institutions Alexios created can meaningfully restrain his heirs and eventually Alexios himself.
- Social consequences of credit growth, banking concentration, dynatoi capitalism and regional winners/losers.
- Integration backlash from both Orthodox Romans and newly subjected Muslim/Turkic populations.
