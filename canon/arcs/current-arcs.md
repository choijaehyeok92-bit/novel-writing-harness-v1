# Arc Canon Through Chapter 107

## Arc 1 — Survival, Regency, Andronikos (ch1–26) — COMPLETED

### Core question
Can a child emperor who knows his historical murder survive without becoming another palace tyrant?

### Locked beats
- 1180-08-01 possession/transfer.
- Manuel I is persuaded to divide and constrain regency power rather than concentrate it.
- Alexios builds parallel anchors in Church, army, navy, bureaucracy and information.
- Venetian/Latin question becomes a deliberate attempt to prevent the historical anti-Latin spiral.
- Court enemies and Andronikos exploit real grievances.
- Karax battle breaks Andronikos militarily.
- Hippodrome trial lets Andronikos articulate true systemic problems; Alexios concedes them and steals his reform mantle.
- Andronikos executed; collaborators punished; Alexios recognizes that victory creates a debt to actually reform the problems he admitted.

### Character result
Alexios moves from "survive three years" to "become the ruler capable of changing the system."

## Arc 2 — Reform State and Norman War (ch27–48) — COMPLETED, DATE CLEANUP OPEN

### Core question
Can Alexios turn future knowledge into institutions quickly enough to survive the 1185 strategic crisis?

### Locked beats
- tax/land/accounting reform and new advisory talent.
- paper, arithmetic/bookkeeping, technical workshop, printing.
- old elite resistance shifts from court intrigue to legal/political organization.
- merit recruitment, army command restructuring, fortress redesign.
- Angelos/Palaiologos conspiracy and Philadelphia disturbance contained.
- Norman invasion defeated decisively around Dyrrachium; Alexios wins overwhelming legitimacy.
- Sicily forced into reparations/constraints; Serbia resubordinated.
- postwar legitimacy spent on 100-seat Synkletos, 166-seat Syllogos architecture, nine-logothesion cabinet, high-court reform, university expansion.
- Cheirobolon reaches operational reliability by 1187-08-04.

### Open continuity issue
The manuscript's explicit years around ch26–40 contradict chapter order. Keep event order but do not invent a corrected calendar until author decision.

## Arc 3 — Two Romes and the Great Crusade (ch49–93) — COMPLETED

### Core question
Can Alexios use the Jerusalem crisis to reconcile enough of East/West to recover Anatolia and the Holy City without surrendering Roman identity?

### Locked beats
- Jerusalem falls; Eastern patriarchal crusade initiative precedes/pressures Western response.
- Syllogos first election and sitting occur under war-tax/Latin anxiety; early parties emerge.
- Diplomatic rapprochement with papacy and Frederick Barbarossa.
- Frederick crosses Roman lands peacefully and survives; Alexios and Frederick formalize a limited "Two Romes" partnership.
- Gunpowder combined formations enter field use.
- Philomelion/Iconium break Rum; Kaykhusraw becomes dependent ruler; direct Roman Anatolian recovery expands.
- Antioch/Cilicia brought into stronger Roman suzerainty arrangements.
- Acre/Damietta/Judean operations break Saladin's strategic position.
- Jerusalem restored by negotiated settlement; massacre prevented; Muslim worship/residence protected under a medieval legal hierarchy.
- Conrad I becomes king; rival western claims are managed.
- Coalition returns with True Cross relics and enormous legitimacy.
- Anna proves herself as long-duration regent.
- Friendship with Frederick transitions into rivalry as western empire learns gunpowder and prepares its own consolidation.

### Character result
Alexios becomes more than a survivor/reformer: he is now a sacral-military "recoverer" whose authority can support constitutional/legal innovations that would previously have been impossible.

## Arc 4 — Succession, Finance, Roman Identity, Integration (ch94–107) — CURRENT

### Core question
Can a state built by one extraordinary emperor survive dynastic succession, financial complexity, religious pluralism and conquest-integration?

### Locked beats
- 1192 succession law codifies designation, order of succession, bicameral ratification, church coronation, army/citizen proclamation, regency, anti-usurpation and anti-mutilation rules.
- Child Manuel becomes co-emperor under the new legitimacy framework.
- Old guard retires/dies; generational change begins.
- Venice/Dandolo probes the new Roman financial system through state-debt pressure; Roman bond-market response holds.
- Logisterion is separated from Ophthalmos to prevent over-concentration of coercive/audit power.
- Spinning-wheel diffusion and Yusuf's promotion force the regime to define a political Roman identity broader than Orthodoxy.
- Four-field agriculture and pasture registration create both prosperity and nomad displacement.
- Alexios deliberately uses the crisis to separate tribes willing to register/contract from those willing to fight; cooperating communities receive protection and economic routes, resistant ones are destroyed.
- Imperial equestrian games convert Anatolian/Turkic martial skill into a shared public spectacle.
- Ch107 endpoint: Anatolikon meros defeats Laskaris meros 4–3 and wins the Diskos tis Tikis.

### Active future pressures already seeded
- Dandolo + Henry / Western imperial coordination against Roman commercial expansion.
- Ayyubid post-Saladin succession.
- Balsamon/Church anxiety over secular political Roman identity.
- Whether institutions Alexios created can meaningfully restrain his heirs and eventually Alexios himself.
- Social consequences of credit growth, banking concentration, dynatoi capitalism and regional winners/losers.
- Integration backlash from both Orthodox Romans and newly subjected Muslim/Turkic populations.
