## Current canonical endpoint

- Latest reviewed manuscript: **Chapter 123, `무엇을 남길 것인가 (2)`**.
- Richard's 1199 victory over Philip II is now fully connected to the earlier anti-Capetian coalition, gunpowder adaptation, survival past Châlus, Bouvines maneuver and post-battle settlement.

## Richard's road to Bouvines

- Andeli alliance with Baldwin IX.
- Anti-Capetian alignment expands to include Renaud of Boulogne and other lords named in the manuscript.
- 1198 Gisors becomes a severe French defeat.
- Both sides use gunpowder; Richard's edge is operational experience rather than monopoly.
- Richard develops limited early field artillery after observing Roman use at Damietta.
- Philip's loss weakens willingness to rebel against Richard in Limousin and the historical Châlus death does not occur.
- Papal mediation continues but has less urgency because Jerusalem is already recovered.

## 1199 Bouvines

- Philip tries to solve the two-front crisis by forcing a decision against Flanders.
- Richard masks his northern maneuver behind continued Normandy pressure.
- Flemish guides and relay horses help Richard appear in Philip's eastern/southeastern rear.
- Philip retreats toward Lille/Artois.
- His rear is caught at Bouvines while crossing the Marque.
- Richard acts as effective supreme commander of the coalition.
- Baldwin holds the French rear; Richard's cavalry takes the western bridge exit; Renaud/mercenaries add rear/escape pressure.
- Philip personally attempts a breakout.
- His horse is struck by a stray gunshot.
- Philip falls, is surrounded and surrenders alive.

## Settlement

Richard uses the victory to secure dynastic structure rather than to take the French crown.

He obtains:
- permanent French royal renunciation of overlordship and associated feudal/jurisdictional rights over Normandy, Anjou, Maine, Touraine, Poitou, Aquitaine and Gascony;
- recognition of Edward as heir;
- Gisors and principal Vexin castle/frontier terms;
- protection of Flanders/Boulogne and other supporting lords;
- a ban on French support/protection for Angevin rebels;
- SECRET: 15-year exclusion of France from Henry VI's anti-English / anti-Eastern-Roman military alignment;
- 40,000 marks in immediate/installment obligations;
- castle transfer and first payment before Philip's release;
- princely hostages and joint guarantees;
- papal/clerical witnessing and oaths from Louis/major princes;
- a minimum 15-year peace tied to Edward's maturation.

## Political result

- The listed continental Angevin lands effectively exit French royal overlordship.
- They are **not** established as ordinary administrative territory of England.
- The Capetian monarchy survives.
- Richard has not yet adopted a formal imperial title.
- Edward's inheritance is now supported by a major international settlement rather than only Richard's internal succession planning.

## Henry VI

- The western setback changes Henry's strategic calculus.
- His immediate on-page conclusion is to attack Eastern Rome — a "강행 돌파."
- The exact sequence of diplomacy, mobilization and any negotiation with Richard remains unresolved.
