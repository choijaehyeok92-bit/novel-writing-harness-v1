# Timeline Addendum — Chapter 123

## 1197 — Andeli alignment

- Richard I and Baldwin IX of Flanders pledge joint resistance to Philip II at Andeli.
- Richard uses economic coercion and inducement to bring Flanders firmly into the anti-Capetian alignment.
- Renaud de Dammartin of Boulogne and other lords including Brabant, Perche and Blois align with Richard.
- Baldwin attacks Artois and takes Aire and Saint-Omer.

## 1198-09 — Gisors

- Richard defeats Philip much more decisively than in the historical baseline.
- Both sides field gunpowder weapons.
- France receives western-imperial gunpowder military support.
- Richard has developed limited field artillery through his royal workshops:
  - lighter/shorter cannon,
  - smaller caliber,
  - reinforced-cart transport,
  - fixed emplacement for firing,
  - limited pre-battle rather than free intra-battle mobility.
- Richard uses an ambush bombardment.
- Philip escapes after a bridge collapse during retreat.
- Richard uses the phrase "신과 나의 권리다!"

## 1198–1199 — political aftershock

- Previously hesitant lords move toward Richard or avoid hostility.
- The viscount of Limousin chooses accommodation.
- The historical Châlus chain does not occur; Richard survives.
- Innocent III still mediates but does not have the same crusade-driven urgency as in the historical baseline.

## 1199 — French northern offensive

- Philip attempts to break the two-front pressure by attacking toward Flanders.
- He seeks to cut the Tournai–Lille–Artois connection.
- Baldwin maintains pressure in Artois; Renaud of Boulogne is active.
- Richard masks his main movement behind continued pressure from the Normandy front.
- With Flemish guides and relay horses, he moves rapidly north through Boulogne/Flemish territory and threatens Philip's eastern/southeastern rear.
- Philip withdraws toward Lille–Artois.

## 1199 — Battle of Bouvines

- Richard catches Philip's rear while the French army is crossing the Marque at Bouvines.
- Richard acts as effective coalition supreme commander.
- Disposition:
  - left: Flanders/Baldwin IX,
  - right: Boulogne,
  - center: Richard and his knights,
  - mercenaries + Renaud: rear/escape pressure.
- Some French troops cross; Philip, royal knights and rear remain on the eastern side.
- Richard's elite cavalry seizes the western exit of the bridge.
- Philip attempts a mounted breakout.
- A stray gunshot hits Philip's horse; Philip falls.
- The royal knights are surrounded.
- Philip orders surrender and becomes Richard's prisoner.

## 1199 — imposed settlement

- French royal overlordship over Normandy, Anjou, Maine, Touraine, Poitou, Aquitaine and Gascony is renounced for Philip and his successors.
- Edward is recognized by Philip and Louis as Richard's lawful heir to the cross-Channel inheritance.
- Gisors and principal Vexin castles pass to Richard; frontier/castle ownership is to be fixed.
- Richard's allied lords receive non-retaliation, recognized agreed rights/lands and prisoner-release guarantees.
- France may not support or shelter rebels against England/Angevin continental lands.
- SECRET: Philip is barred for at least 15 years from joining Henry VI's anti-English / anti-Eastern-Roman military alignment.
- Payment: 40,000 marks in immediate and installment obligations.
- Release preconditions: transfer of key castles + first payment; thereafter princely hostages/joint guarantees.
- Papal and senior clerical witnessing; Louis and major French princes swear.
- At least 15 years of peace, linked to Edward's maturation.

## Immediate geopolitical result

- The continental Angevin lands from Normandy through Aquitaine effectively exit French royal overlordship.
- The Capetian monarchy survives.
- No formal Angevin imperial title is established in this chapter.
- Henry VI responds by deciding to accelerate/directly pursue war against Eastern Rome.
