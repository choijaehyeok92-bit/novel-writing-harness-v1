# Chapter 113 Canon — 시칠리아

> Source: author-approved reviewed manuscript `manuscripts/reviewed/113.시칠리아.txt`. Only facts established on-page are locked below. Forecasts and future thresholds remain plans unless later chapters enact them.

## Senior command-and-staff education

- The first **Taxis ton Strategon / 탁시스 톤 스트라티곤** senior command-and-staff course is shown in operation.
- Alexios's instructional doctrine explicitly links war to political purpose: commanders study Roman military history, Alexios's own `전쟁론`, imperial administration, taxation, mobilization, military law, war finance, logistics, Skepsis intelligence analysis, and command of large organizations.
- The course includes a continuous hypothetical war exercise lasting four months. The scenario evolves rather than resetting: enemy landing, allied or hostile political movements, naval developments, supply crises and operational maneuver force officers to reassess intelligence, revise plans, recalculate logistics and issue new orders.
- **Alexios Gidos** and **Kaloyan** are shown giving different operational answers. Gidos favors preserving the main force, holding Dyrrachium, securing Via Egnatia routes/bridges/depots, forming reserves around Thessaloniki and avoiding major battle before enemy strength is known. Kaloyan favors stripping food/horses from exposed areas, using light cavalry against reconnaissance and foraging, and drawing the enemy inland toward a Roman-chosen battlefield.
- Their disagreement becomes part of the educational method: commanders debate, absorb one another's methods and later demonstrate cross-learning in large-scale exercises.
- The final two months are organized around specialist fields including cavalry war, siege war, logistics, naval war, frontier war, gunpowder war and intelligence war, culminating in large red/blue team exercises.

## Field staff organization — ESTABLISHED

- The **Sekreton tou Stratopedou / 세크레톤 투 스트라토페두** (field/camp secretariat; rendered in Korean as `야전 사무국`) is now being organized for field headquarters.
- Each field headquarters is assigned six staff functions:
  1. personnel/rolls,
  2. intelligence,
  3. operations,
  4. logistics,
  5. engineering and weapons,
  6. documents and communications.
- Command principle is explicit: **the commander decides; staff officers support the decision by making its consequences visible and executable.**
- This is the first on-page establishment of an enduring field staff framework. It is still an embryonic Roman staff system, not a modern numbered G-staff or fully mature General Staff.

## Tagma reorganization — ESTABLISHED CURRENT STRUCTURE

- The standing central army is being standardized around target organizational sizes:
  - smallest squad-like group: about 10–12;
  - company-like grouping: about 50–60;
  - hundred-strength grouping: about 100–120;
  - **allagion**: about 300–400, an independent tactical unit;
  - **tagma**: about 900–1,200;
  - **megas tagma**: about 3,000–4,500, created by combining several branch-centered tagmata into a combined-arms field grouping;
  - **stratopedon**: about 8,000–15,000, an operational army grouping formed from multiple megala tagmata and supporting elements.
- Individual tagmata remain primarily branch-centered; the **megas tagma** is the principal combined-arms grouping assembled by mixing tagmata and supporting arms.
- Current reformed standing central force is stated as roughly **eight tagmata / about 10,000 men**.
- Forecast only: if fiscal performance continues, planning documents estimate **15–18 tagmata / roughly 20,000 standing troops around 1200**. This is not yet achieved.

## Officer qualification transition — PLANNED THRESHOLDS

- For the next four years, Polemiki Scholi graduates receive an advantage rather than an exclusive monopoly on promotion.
- After that transition, the plan is for **tagma deputy-command level and above** to require either the Polemiki Scholi regular course or serving-officer special course.
- From **1205 onward**, the stated plan is for **megas-tagma-level commanders** and **imperial direct operations staff** normally to complete the **Taxis ton Strategon**.
- These future dates are policy intentions announced in chapter 113, not yet realized outcomes.

## Strategic doctrine

- Alexios explicitly frames the coming military competition with the western empire as a test of institutional quality rather than gunpowder monopoly: `진정한 차이는 화약이 아니라는 걸.`
- The reform approach is integration rather than purge: existing military aristocrats are not expelled, but are progressively drawn into standardized professional education and qualification.

## Western succession escalation

- By chapter 113 **Henry VI has obtained imperial coronation and is now explicitly styled Imperator**. The manuscript states that obtaining the coronation required political struggle with the pope, but does not give an exact coronation date in this chapter.
- **Frederick VI, Duke of Swabia, is now actually confined by Henry VI.** This converts Alexios's earlier prediction into an on-page event.
- Frederick remains personally loyal to Henry and denies any treason. Henry explicitly acknowledges that Frederick is innocent and that even the suspicious correspondence contains no proof of conspiracy.
- Henry's reason for confinement is political rather than judicial: Frederick's freedom allows discontented princes to gather around a crusade hero and alternative Hohenstaufen focal point. Henry also acknowledges Frederick's closeness to the eastern Basileus as one factor.
- Henry frames confinement as protecting Frederick from the suspicion and danger his political usefulness creates; he states that the alternative could have been assassination by poison.

## Sicilian war opening

- After learning of Frederick VI's confinement, Alexios interprets it as the beginning of the expected western escalation and orders Sicily warned to prepare for war.
- Henry orders the **ministeriales, imperial knights and major princes** summoned for an invasion of Sicily.
- Henry explicitly frames the campaign as recovery of the hereditary right of his wife/Western Augusta against the surviving Tancred-William III regime.
- Chapter 113 ends with the **Sicilian war beginning**. The actual campaign, naval balance, conquest results and the broader Two-Romes war are not yet resolved on-page.
