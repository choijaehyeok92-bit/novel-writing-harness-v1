# Chapter 115 Canon — 산통

> Source: author-approved reviewed manuscript `manuscripts/reviewed/115.산통.txt`. Only facts established on-page are locked below. The final 1196 narration is a foreshadowed geopolitical trajectory, not yet a completed proxy war.

## Imperial family — 1195

- The year is explicitly **1195**.
- Anna gives birth safely in the purple birth chamber to **twins: one son and one daughter**.
- Both are born in the purple and are explicitly hailed as a **Porphyrogennetos** and **Porphyrogeniti**.
- The son is named **Ioannes / 요안니스**, after Alexios's grandfather.
- The daughter is named **Maria / 마리아**, after Alexios's mother.
- Manuel is present outside the birth chamber with Alexios and then meets the twins. He is already noticeably precocious for his age.
- The scene reinforces Alexios's dynastic motivation: he promises his children a world in which they can live safely, even at the cost of everything he has.

## Maternal-health reform — ESTABLISHED BY 1195

- By 1195 Alexios has implemented empire-level maternal-health measures rather than only private palace precautions.
- Midwives are required to wash their hands; people who have handled corpses or suppurating wounds are barred from approaching mothers without appropriate hygiene separation.
- Midwives from across the empire have been gathered and organized as **imperially certified midwives**.
- Pregnancy management records include previous births, prior obstructed labor, previous major hemorrhage, miscarriage, suspected twins, fetal movement, persistent bleeding, severe edema, severe headache, visual disturbance, fever and late-pregnancy fetal position.
- First births, prior obstructed labor, prior postpartum hemorrhage and recurrent pregnancy bleeding can trigger referral to specialist hospitals on a midwife's judgment under revised law.
- **Bimanual uterine compression** has been introduced as a response to postpartum hemorrhage.
- Criteria exist for **embryotomy/fetal destructive delivery** when the mother is dying from obstructed labor and the fetus is already dead.
- Alexios and Manuel themselves wash with soap and clean poured water before entering the birth chamber, showing that the hygiene regime is treated as an enforced court norm rather than rhetoric.

## Venice — prosperity and strategic anxiety

- Venice is described as enjoying an extraordinary commercial boom rather than economic collapse.
- The boom is driven by more than a decade of low-kommerkion policy in major Roman cities, profitable trade in Roman silk and eastern spices, and expanded Levant trade through Tyre, Antioch and Tripoli within Rome's sphere of influence.
- This prosperity is the central political paradox of the chapter: Venetian commercial success under Roman-led trade makes sections of the Venetian elite fear strategic dependence on Constantinople.

## Venetian political split — ESTABLISHED

- **Pietro Ziani** emerges as the principal voice demanding that Doge **Enrico Dandolo** explain why he is not resolving tensions with Rome.
- Ziani is identified as Sebastiano Ziani's son and as a man whose wealth and client network could have made him a highly plausible dogal contender in 1192.
- **Domenico Sanudo** supports the conciliatory argument and urges Venice to clarify that the Otranto confrontation was not intended as a war with Rome.
- **Pietro Michiel** and **Ottaviano Quirino** also align with Ziani against Dandolo's course.
- The text establishes an identifiable Venetian political cleavage, but it should be treated as a coalition of interests and leading men rather than a modern disciplined party unless later chapters formalize it further.

## Dandolo's anti-Roman doctrine — ESTABLISHED

- Dandolo argues that Venice's problem is not current poverty but **dangerous economic dependence on a powerful imperial market**.
- He invokes the memory of the **1171 mass seizure/arrest of Venetians and Venetian property under Manuel I** as evidence of the vulnerability created by dependence on one emperor's policy.
- His strategic argument is that Alexios is more commercially rational than Manuel, but that this makes dependence more dangerous rather than less: Roman prosperity can bind Venetian capital to Constantinople before Venice realizes it has lost leverage.
- Dandolo explicitly contrasts Venice's constrained dogal government with the much greater personal power available to the Basileus, especially a ruler strengthened by the prestige of recovering the Holy Land.
- He argues that war must be considered **before** Rome becomes too dominant to resist, not after.

## Grammation and Roman commercial standards — ESTABLISHED / EMERGING

- The term **`grammation / γραμμάτιον`** is explicitly used for Rome's paper **gold-convertible monetary certificate/currency**.
- Dandolo does **not** claim that the grammation has already become the uncontested Mediterranean standard. He warns that it is gradually moving in that direction and says that, if Venice remains passive, such a future is obvious.
- Venetian merchants are already using **Roman-required standardized shipping boxes/containers** when trading inside the empire.
- Dandolo therefore frames Roman influence as a system combining money, standards, market access and naval force rather than as simple tariff competition.

## Otranto aftermath in Venetian politics

- The chapter explicitly carries the chapter-114 Otranto incident into domestic Venetian politics.
- Dandolo treats the Roman fleet's ability to check Venice and Genoa/Pisa's decision to turn away as evidence that the strategic conflict has already begun.
- Ziani rejects the conclusion that Venice must therefore prepare for war, warning that direct confrontation could destroy Venetian privileges and hand Roman markets to Genoa and Pisa.
- Ziani accuses Dandolo of placing Venetian citizens on the table as stakes in a dangerous gamble.
- The meeting ends with Ziani and his supporters leaving the Great Council chamber; narration describes this as **Venice splitting in two** politically.

## 1196 Balkan succession trigger — FORESHADOWED, NOT YET RESOLVED

- The chapter then advances to **1196**.
- **Béla III of Hungary dies.**
- **Stefan Nemanja abdicates** the Serbian grand-princely position to his second son **Stefan Nemanjić** and withdraws to monastic life.
- Narration explicitly states that these two successions will soon become battlefields in which the eastern and western Roman empires point their weapons at one another.
- This locks the coming **Hungarian and Serbian succession struggles as the next proxy-war arena**, but chapter 115 does not yet establish the exact alliances, interventions, battle dates or final outcomes.
