## Western proxy-war network

- After Béla III's death and Stefan Nemanja's withdrawal to monastic life, Henry VI accelerates a multi-front anti-Roman strategy.
- Andrew (언드라시), Béla III's second son, refuses to accept Emeric's succession and seeks the Hungarian crown.
- Leopold VI of Styria approaches Andrew as Henry VI's intermediary and promises German support.
- Andrew agrees to use claims over Croatia and Dalmatia as the political basis for rebellion.
- Anti-Roman Serbian nobles rally around Vukan, Stefan Nemanja's eldest son, against Stefan Nemanjić.
- Vukan allies with Andrew rather than with Emeric as in the historical baseline.
- The papacy refuses to back Vukan against the eastern Roman Empire and instead recognizes Emeric's legitimate Hungarian kingship.
- Philip II of France joins Henry's wider anti-Roman alignment because Richard I has returned as a crusade hero, the Angevin realm remains strong, and Alexios maintains close relations with Richard.
- Henry offers Cilician Armenia's Levon full independence and, after Conrad of Querfurt's advice, an additional advantage regarding Antioch in order to pull him away from Roman suzerainty.
- Conrad of Querfurt is established in-scene as Henry's imperial chancellor and bishop of Hildesheim.

## Anatolian proxy front

- Qutb al-Din Malikshah has survived in this timeline, having fled Egypt after Saladin's defeat and then sheltered among anti-Roman groups still present in the Seljuk sphere.
- Henry, using Venetian mediation, sends Malikshah a proposal to create pressure in Rome's Anatolian rear.
- Malikshah accepts because he still regards the sultanate as rightfully his.
- Kaykhusraw's regime is acknowledged in-story as competent but structurally weakened by being installed under Roman power; many amirs obey largely because they fear Rome.

## Venetian strategy

- Dandolo orders his family and aligned merchants to:
  - sell Roman `황은채`,
  - refuse extensions on Roman merchants' bills,
  - remit silver back to Venice,
  - raise maritime-insurance premiums,
  - reduce transport of grain and timber.
- Dandolo's objective is not merely immediate profit but to provoke confrontation and persuade Venetians that Roman growth threatens republican autonomy.
- Venice offers Andrew recognition and naval support in exchange for:
  - Venetian rights and privileges at Zadar,
  - free navigation,
  - exclusion of eastern Roman fleets and garrisons from Dalmatia.
- Andrew accepts while privately intending to reconsider the bargain after gaining the crown.
- Dandolo likewise treats Zadar as a bargaining instrument; his larger aim is exclusion of Roman influence from the Adriatic.

## Opening of the multi-front war

- The anti-Roman coalition begins coordinated pressure from **1196 onward**.
- Andrew rebels and Emeric loses at Mački.
- Vukan's supporters rebel against Stefan Nemanjić in Serbia.
- Malikshah and supporting amirs threaten Caesarea.
- Levon mobilizes in Cilician Armenia.
- France increases military preparations without yet openly moving.
- Venetian shipping and ports support Andrew and Vukan with money and matériel.
- Alexios orders accelerated aid to Emeric and Stefan Nemanjić:

  - military advisers,
  - crossbows,
  - limited gunpowder weapons,
  - Skepsis support,
  - supply preparations,
  - Tourkopouloi,
  - Roman naval action against Venetian ships carrying rebel military supplies.
- Alexios orders Kaykhusraw to prepare for the possible abandonment of Caesarea if necessary and strengthens the eastern Roman military posture.
