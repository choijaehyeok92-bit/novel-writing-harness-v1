# Chapter 117 Canon — 양 로마 대전 (2)

> Source: author-approved reviewed manuscript `manuscripts/reviewed/117.양 로마 대전 (2).txt`.
> Only on-page facts are locked. Exact year is not restated in this chapter; do not assign one solely from later planning discussion.

## Hungarian front — STALEMATE

- Andrew initially defeats Emeric at Mački and gains Croatia and Dalmatia.
- Emeric, reinforced by a Roman force under **Alexios Gidos**, counterattacks near Lake Balaton and defeats Andrew's army.
- German mercenaries are established as Andrew's core military component in this campaign.
- The Hungarian front hardens around the **Drava crossing zone**:
  - Andrew controls much of Croatia, Dalmatia and Slavonia.
  - Emeric retains the Hungarian heartland and central/northern Pannonia.
- Andrew's offensive momentum is broken rather than his faction being destroyed.

## Serbian front — STALEMATE

- Vukan rises from Zeta with support flowing through Henry, Andrew and Venice.
- Some Hum forces join him and he initially drives Stefan's side back in western highlands and the Lim sector.
- Because Serbia is a direct Roman vassal, Rome sends a tagma under **Kaloyan** to reinforce Grand Župan Stefan Nemanjić.
- Vukan's army is heavily defeated at Raška by Stefan's army with Roman support and gunpowder weapons.
- Inland Serbian opinion is not uniformly anti-Roman; the manuscript explicitly contrasts inland populations with coastal Duklja/Zeta and portrays Vukan's closeness to the Roman Church as another reason some inland Serbs reject him.
- The Serbian front hardens along a zone described as:
  - upper Lim,
  - west of Ras,
  - the Ibar valley,
  - **Zvečan**.
- Zvečan becomes the principal symbolic barrier to Vukan's advance.
- Andrew and Vukan become heavily dependent on continuing western money and supplies.

## Adriatic escalation

- Rome attempts to interdict Venetian shipping carrying supplies toward Vukan through Croatian and Dalmatian ports.
- The Roman fleet advances into the Adriatic and attempts seizures.
- Venice responds with its own fleet; Roman admiral **Konstantinos Angelos Doukas** refuses an unnecessary full battle near Venice's maritime sphere and withdraws after making the warning clear.
- Alexios rejects Venetian merchant protests in Constantinople while deliberately avoiding a general expulsion or mass arrest of Venetians.
- The incident therefore escalates Roman-Venetian hostility without yet becoming a general naval war.

## Anatolian front — STALEMATE

- Malikshah re-emerges at **Sivas**, where local amirs shelter and support him.
- With German and Venetian backing, his coalition advances on **Caesarea**.
- He fails to take the city.
- The manuscript explains the failure structurally:
  - his coalition is strong in mobile warfare, raiding and rural control;
  - it is weak in cohesion, logistics and siege warfare.
- Kaykhusraw remains inside Caesarea and chooses endurance rather than open battle.
- Rome supplies the city with limited gunpowder weapons, crossbowmen, technicians and food; a Roman tagma is already stationed there.
- Malikshah also faces attacks on his rear from a Roman-vassal Danishmendid polity centered around **Niksar–Tokat–Amasya**.
- The manuscript spells this house/group as **`즐눈계`** and states that the founder/ancestor 즐눈 is dead while his family has recovered part of its former Danishmendid authority under Roman victory.
- These vassals raid Sivas's surroundings, depots, livestock and communications, pinning Malikshah between Caesarea and his rear.

## Cilician Armenian front — STALEMATE

- The Lampron Hetumids rebel against Levon and refuse to join rebellion against the Roman basileus.
- Their uprising cannot destroy Levon and the Rubenids but does pin a substantial portion of Levon's field forces in the Taurus.
- Lampron functions as the key mountain fortress enabling this resistance.
- Levon is therefore unable to move freely toward Antioch; his remaining forces are also constrained by fear of a Roman intervention.

## Western front opens

- Henry's attempt to impose multiple fronts on Rome has not broken the Roman system; local allies, vassals and limited Roman deployments absorb the pressure.
- Richard I then sends Philip II a final ultimatum.
- Richard's jurists use Henry II's refusal to receive homage from a crowned co-king as precedent for the argument that an anointed king should not be another king's vassal.
- Richard publicly rejects the political logic of one crowned king kneeling to another.
- In the formal argument he accuses Philip of first violating feudal good faith by courting Angevin vassals and threatening Richard's rights, then renounces service to Philip as lord of Normandy, Anjou and Aquitaine.
- Philip answers by treating Richard as a rebellious Duke of Normandy whose fiefs can be confiscated.
- Philip seeks Henry VI's support under their prior agreement.
- Henry promises:
  - silver and bills,
  - permission to recruit knights and mercenaries in the Rhine and Lorraine regions,
  - ministeriales,
  - crossbowmen,
  - siege engineers,
  - a limited quantity of gunpowder weapons,
  - transit and supply cooperation from friendly imperial lords,
  - political pressure on princes close to Richard.

## Venetian financial-war transition

- The Adriatic confrontation helps Dandolo win over part of the Venetian Great Council's middle faction.
- Dandolo then prepares a renewed financial assault against Roman banking and credit.
- Chapter 117 ends on the thesis that war can be waged with gold and paper as well as weapons, with Dandolo ordering Venetians acting under the Republic's command to move against the imperial `trapezai`.
