# Chapter 119 Canon — 성 마르코 공황 (2)

> Source: author-approved reviewed manuscript `manuscripts/reviewed/119.성 마르코 공황 (2).txt`.
> Only on-page facts are locked. Debate outcomes and measures not yet enacted remain unresolved.

## Crisis administration

- The Saint Mark Panic spreads beyond markets into the Roman state apparatus itself: Koini Trapeza and the finance administration initially experience confusion because no one except Alexios has ever lived through a financial panic of this scale.
- **Elias ben Mordecai** takes the lead inside Koini Trapeza.
- **Megas Logothetes Choniates** restores order inside the finance administration and orders an immediate consolidated report on:
  - treasury balances,
  - tax revenues,
  - emergency resources,
  - Logisterion findings,
  - Ophthalmos intelligence,
  - Skepsis analysis,
  - Demosios Dromos communications.
- Synkletos and Syllogos leadership, as well as Chrysozonoi and Kyanophoroi leadership, are alerted for emergency parliamentary action.
- Logisterion proactively prepares to audit the large trapezai before the formal imperial order arrives.

## Venetian attack investigation — established / action still pending

- Roman intelligence traces the opening of the panic to simultaneous actions by multiple Venetian-affiliated merchant houses:
  - sales of `황은채`,
  - refusal to extend maturing bills.
- Many Venetian houses are reported to be withdrawing from the empire.
- Ophthalmos prepares to recommend:
  - exit restrictions on Venetians,
  - searches of remaining Venetian merchant houses.
- **These recommendations are not enacted on-page in chapter 119.**
- Skepsis assesses the activity as a coordinated Venetian attack, likely intended to shake the Roman economy and thereby weaken Roman support for European and Anatolian client/allied fronts.
- Roman officials discuss the possibility that the attack could justify revocation of Venetian privileges and mutual treaty arrangements, but no such revocation is enacted in this chapter.

## Synkletos and Syllogos mobilization

- Synkletos issues an emergency summons and its leadership sees the Syllogos's slower mobilization as a possible opportunity to regain initiative.
- Syllogos also mobilizes urgently.
- All provincial members would require roughly two weeks to arrive, but because a regular session is approaching, a quorum is expected sooner.
- Chrysozonoi expect almost all of their members within roughly one week.
- Kyanophoroi expect roughly two-thirds of their own members in the same window.
- Chapter 119 does **not** newly define the exact constitutional quorum percentage.
- An emergency plenary session is to be called as soon as the emperor orders it.

## Emergency financial diagnostics

- Alexios orders Koini Trapeza and Logisterion to complete a simplified balance-sheet survey of the major trapezai in **one day**.
- Required items include:
  - gold and silver,
  - grammation,
  - demand deposits,
  - `황은채`,
  - commercial bills,
  - accepted/guaranteed bills,
  - maritime loans,
  - claims on and obligations to other trapezai.
- Alexios explicitly orders officials to examine **maturity structure**, not only total assets.
- The survey distinguishes:
  - institutions that remain fundamentally sound but lack immediate cash,
  - institutions that are effectively unable to pay and must enter resolution.

## Chrysocheir resolution

- The Chrysocheir network is not uniformly insolvent; most affiliated trapezai remain fundamentally sound but illiquid.
- Some affiliated trapezai are effectively insolvent, and smaller trapezai are expected to contain more failures.
- Under an **imperial emergency edict**, Leon Chrysocheir agrees to:
  - suspension of his management rights,
  - use of his equity as first-loss capital,
  - collateralization of personal property within the scope of his guarantee responsibility.
- Sound/viable functions are transferred into a **new temporary trapeza**:
  - collectible commercial bills,
  - performing maritime loans,
  - deposit accounts,
  - payment functions.
- Speculative loans, bad guarantees and impaired investments are transferred to a **liquidation estate**.
- The failed old Chrysocheir entity is removed from central clearing; the temporary Chrysocheir institution replaces it.
- The chapter therefore establishes a Roman prototype of bank resolution that preserves payment functions while imposing losses on the owner.

## Koini Trapeza as lender of last resort

- Koini Trapeza begins emergency lending to designated trapezai against collateral including:
  - `황은채`,
  - sound commercial bills,
  - reliable maritime claims,
  - warehouse or commodity collateral.
- Collateral is valued conservatively with substantial haircuts.
- Emergency loans carry an interest rate above the normal Koini Trapeza rate.
- This is explicitly presented as Koini Trapeza performing its intended **lender-of-last-resort** function.

## 황은채 stabilization

- Alexios rejects simple mass repurchase of `황은채` by the treasury because that could merely convert Roman gold into an exit route for Venetian sellers.
- Instead, Koini Trapeza recognizes `황은채` as emergency collateral.
- Example emergency valuation established on-page:
  - market price near 60% of face,
  - emergency collateral value at 70% of face.
- This preferential emergency valuation applies **only to holdings recorded before the panic**, explicitly to prevent arbitrage.
- The 70% treatment is defined as an **emergency valuation**, not a permanent guarantee.
- The purpose is to stop forced sales, stabilize collateral values and reverse the forced-sale / margin-pressure spiral.
- By the **fourth day of the panic**, the decline in `황은채` prices stops in the Constantinople market and prices begin to rebound.
- Provincial markets such as Thessaloniki, Nicaea and Antioch are expected to stabilize later, with a lag of days to weeks via the Demosios Dromos information network.

## Central clearing and net settlement

- Alexios orders all inter-trapeza payments routed through Koini Trapeza central clearing.
- Settlement is conducted once daily on a **net basis**, reducing the amount of physical cash that must move through the system.
- This does not erase Venetian claims, but materially weakens the liquidity pressure generated by simultaneous gross-cash demands.
- The old failed Chrysocheir entity is excluded from the clearing network and replaced by the temporary institution, limiting contagion from its unpaid obligations.

## Grammation and protected accounts

- Small-denomination / ordinary grammation convertibility is fully maintained.
- Large commercial conversions are centralized through Koini Trapeza so that payment sequencing can be managed.
- The chapter explicitly states that this is **not a refusal to redeem**, but management of payment order.
- Inter-trapeza payments are netted on the books.
- Alexios orders protection for:
  - small personal deposits,
  - wage/payroll accounts,
  - merchant settlement accounts.
- The objective is to preserve public belief that grammation remains convertible into gold.

## Fiscal limit and parliamentary turn

- The finance administration reports that the amount required for the crisis exceeds the limits of the current budget.
- Alexios orders an **emergency supplementary budget** sent to the urgently summoned Syllogos.
- The manuscript states that, under the current legal doctrine, the emperor could act by direct authority, but Alexios deliberately chooses Syllogos involvement because otherwise its creation would be meaningless and because he wants to demonstrate institutional resilience to foreign enemies.
- The supplementary budget is **not passed on-page** in chapter 119.
- No amount, funding source, duration, deposit-loss guarantee or final fiscal burden is yet established.

## Chapter endpoint

- The chapter ends with the Roman system having passed the technical/administrative phase of crisis response and now facing its final political test:
  - **“의회라는 문 앞에서.”**
- The outcome of the emergency parliamentary process remains unresolved.
