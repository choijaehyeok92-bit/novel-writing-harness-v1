# Chapter 121 Canon — 공화국을 위하여

> Source: author-approved reviewed manuscript `manuscripts/reviewed/121.공화국을 위하여.txt`.
> Only on-page facts are locked. Later legal justification, public reaction, and the final fate of the Ziani faction remain unresolved.

## Constantinople — Venetian protest fails

- The Venetian **bailo** in Constantinople protests the Roman retaliation to Logothetes tou Dromou **Basilios Kamateros Doukas**.
- In this alternate-history timeline, the bailo is an already-established treaty office; reviewers must not flag the title merely because the historical office is better documented later.
- Basilios warns that Rome has deliberately refrained from repeating Manuel I's 1171 mass arrest of Venetians.
- Ophthalmos searches Venetian merchant houses tied to the opening phase of the Saint Mark Panic.
- Roman officials produce:
  - a coded message from Venice connected to the Saint Mark Day financial operation,
  - a decoded version,
  - the corresponding decryption key/table.
- The bailo can no longer credibly maintain that Rome has no evidence of organized Venetian involvement.
- Basilios delivers Alexios's warning: Rome does not seek war, but Venice must be prepared to risk everything if it chooses war.

## Dandolo's strategic reasoning

- Enrico Dandolo acknowledges that the Roman response was faster and more institutionally coherent than he expected.
- He nevertheless concludes that this failure proves his underlying strategic fear:
  - if Venice waits, the power gap between Rome and Venice will become irreversible.
- Dandolo does **not** trust Henry VI or the Holy Roman Empire as a permanent partner.
- His reason for using the western emperor is strategic dependence:
  - in the current timeline Henry holds mainland southern Italy but does not possess the Hauteville/Sicilian naval system concentrated on the island;
  - Henry therefore lacks an independent maritime system comparable to Venice and must rely on maritime partners while rebuilding.
- Dandolo's intended end state is to use the western empire against eastern Rome and later use Venice's lagoon, shipping, and finance to constrain the western emperor.
- After the Saint Mark Panic fails, Dandolo believes he personally remains the only Venetian leader who understands the urgency of this strategic danger.
- His internal logic shifts from persuasion to coercion: if Venetians do not understand, they must be made to understand.

## Ziani opposition coalition

- **Pietro Ziani** becomes the focus of an anti-Dandolo coalition.
- Named participants include:
  - Domenico Sanudo,
  - Pietro Michiel,
  - Ottaviano Querini.
- Their motives are mixed:
  - commercial loss,
  - patriotism,
  - political ambition,
  - fear that Dandolo is driving Venice toward ruin.
- They judge Dandolo's fixation on the Eastern Empire as destructive rather than protective.

## Planned constitutional removal of Dandolo

- Ziani explicitly invokes recent precedents of ducal resignation:
  - his father,
  - Dandolo's immediate predecessor.
- The opposition does not plan a modern impeachment procedure.
- It plans to make Dandolo politically unable to govern and then demand his resignation.
- The chapter establishes the following Venetian constitutional assumptions for the current timeline:
  - the doge cannot freely decide major state policy alone;
  - the **Minor Council** is treated as a six-member body;
  - Great Council and Minor Council support are essential for major policy;
  - Dandolo's Saint Mark operation was conducted indirectly through aligned merchants/merchant houses rather than through an openly approved state act.
- Ziani's faction argues that the Saint Mark Day operation occurred without Great Council approval and therefore violated Dandolo's sworn limits.
- At the meeting:
  - at least three of six Minor Council members are described as already aligned with the opposition;
  - two more are described as uncertain but drifting toward it.
- The opposition plans to:
  - block further anti-Roman measures,
  - deny funding for retaliatory war,
  - demand appointment of negotiators to Rome,
  - finally demand Dandolo's resignation.
- The manuscript frames this route as sufficient to reduce the dogeship to political paralysis even before any formal removal.

## Armed counter-coup

- Before Ziani's faction can execute the plan, armed men storm the Ziani residence.
- The force is:
  - disciplined,
  - violent,
  - visibly non-local in speech,
  - marked by a clear German accent.
- A servant is struck down and the assembled Venetian elites are surrounded.
- The armed men say the nobles are being "retired" rather than killed.
- They accuse the opposition of collusion with the Eastern Empire.
- Pietro Ziani recognizes the action as Dandolo's move and denounces him for dragging Venice into a destructive war.
- The intercut immediately shows Dandolo alone in the ducal palace answering, in effect, that everything is being done **for Venice**.
- The narrative therefore establishes Dandolo as the political author of the coercive move even though the mercenaries do not openly announce an imperial or official Venetian chain of command.
- The chapter does **not** yet establish:
  - whether the captives are imprisoned, exiled, forced into monasteries, or otherwise disposed of;
  - whether the German-accented men are directly supplied by Henry VI, privately hired, or recruited through intermediaries;
  - how the Great Council, Minor Council, contrade, or wider populace react once the seizure becomes known.

## Thematic endpoint

- Alexios reflects that Rome and Venice could have coexisted in this timeline.
- He interprets Dandolo's fatal choice as obsession with losing strategic primacy.
- The chapter closes with Alexios consciously framing the coming conflict as a reversal of the original historical fate in which Constantinople burned.
- The thematic contrast is now explicit in events:
  - Alexios responds to crisis by strengthening institutions and voluntarily accepting procedural restraints;
  - Dandolo responds to political defeat by bypassing institutions through coercion while believing he acts for the republic.
