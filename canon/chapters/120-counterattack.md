# Chapter 120 Canon — 반격

> Source: author-approved reviewed manuscript `manuscripts/reviewed/120.반격.txt`.
> Only facts established on-page are locked below. Venetian reactions and the ultimate strategic result remain unresolved.

## Syllogos composition and emergency sitting

- One week after the chapter 119 parliamentary endpoint, the Syllogos reaches a workable quorum.
- The current Syllogos has **216 seats**, expanded beyond the original 166-seat first apportionment after population growth and the incorporation of recovered Bucellarian and Anatolikon territories.
- **165 members** are present at the emergency sitting.
- Although the Kyanophoroi have held the majority since 1196, fewer of their members are physically present than Chrysozonoi members because the Kyanophoroi base is more rural and provincial and many members normally remain in their themes outside session.
- The near approach of the regular session explains why enough provincial members are already traveling toward the capital to produce a quorum after roughly one week.

## Venetian attack condemnation and fiscal debate

- Megas Logothetes **Niketas Choniates / Niketas Akominatos** formally presents the crisis to the Syllogos as a coordinated Venetian attack:
  - simultaneous sales of `황은채`,
  - refusal to extend bills,
  - simultaneous demands for cash repayment.
- He asks the Syllogos to:
  1. condemn the action as an attack on the Romans;
  2. support additional fiscal spending beyond the existing monthly/annual budget.
- The Chrysozonoi react most aggressively, reflecting both their broadly pro-court/expansion line and their urban-merchant constituency's rivalry with Venetian merchants.
- **Asen**, speaking for the Kyanophoroi, supports retaliation against Venice but argues that Roman taxpayers should not socialize losses created by reckless private trapezitai.
- He specifically demands that public money protect depositors, especially small depositors, rather than compensate owners for risky private transactions.
- Choniates answers that insolvent Chrysocheir affiliates are being resolved, Leon's management rights are already under Logisterion control, and the emergency spending is intended primarily for depositor/system protection rather than owner bailout.

## Constitutional bargain — Syllogos budget review

- Asen initially proposes an audit committee placing representatives of both parties and Synkletos personnel directly into supervision of special-budget execution.
- Choniates resists direct legislative participation in administration because it would blur the boundary between the imperial executive/administration and the assemblies.
- In subsequent negotiation, Asen explicitly seeks a precedent guaranteeing meaningful Syllogos influence over public finance so that later emperors cannot easily reduce the body to a disposable petition assembly.
- Alexios agrees with the underlying argument but rejects direct legislative participation in treasury execution.
- Alexios establishes a new constitutional practice:
  - before a budget is finalized, it is to be submitted to the **Syllogos for review**;
  - the Syllogos may issue **recommendations** on the budget;
  - the emperor is **not legally bound** by those recommendations.
- The manuscript explicitly frames this as an intentionally self-binding political precedent rather than a transfer of sovereignty away from the emperor.
- Asen's original proposal to insert legislators directly into treasury execution is rejected.

## Emergency supplementary budget — passed

- All **165 members present vote in favor** of the supplementary-budget support resolution.
- Because this exceeds two-thirds of the full 216-seat Syllogos, the resolution goes directly to the emperor without needing Synkletos review under the already-established procedure for two-thirds Syllogos measures.
- The manuscript treats this as the supplementary budget being effectively approved and additional public resources becoming available for crisis response.
- No exact monetary amount is established on-page.

## Synkletos inquiry role

- Alexios separately gives Synkletos authority to conduct an inquiry/hearing into the financial crisis and failed trapezai.
- Leon Chrysocheir is publicly questioned over whether reckless management contributed to the crisis.
- Synkletos eagerly uses the opportunity to scrutinize failed/private financial actors while avoiding a direct political attack on Alexios or his cabinet.
- This establishes a practical post-crisis political inquiry role for Synkletos distinct from Syllogos budget review and Logisterion professional audit.

## Panic stabilization

- The Saint Mark Panic begins to subside within roughly **one month** under the combined response of the emperor, financial institutions, administration and representative bodies.
- Chapter 120 therefore closes the immediate panic-management phase and opens a retaliatory economic-war phase.

## Venetian retaliation package — enacted

### Escrow / compensation security

- By imperial edict, Venetian receivables inside the empire are diverted into **Koini Trapeza escrow** rather than paid directly to Venetian recipients, including:
  - proceeds from goods sold in the empire,
  - bills owed by Roman merchants,
  - maritime-loan repayments,
  - principal and interest on `황은채`.
- Funds remain withheld until Venice compensates Roman losses from the attack.
- The manuscript explicitly states that escrow **does not transfer ownership**.
- Until compensation is assessed, no one — including the emperor — may dispose of escrowed funds arbitrarily.

### Suspension of Venetian privileges

Pending investigation and compensation negotiations:
- Venetian tax-exemption privileges are suspended.
- Use of Venetian-exclusive quays/warehouses is suspended.
- Financial-attack cases are moved into imperial-court jurisdiction.
- Registration of new Venetian merchant houses and agencies is halted.

### Koini Trapeza restrictions

- Venetian merchant houses lose the right to independent settlement/clearing inside the Roman system.
- Their payments must pass through designated trapezai and be processed by Koini Trapeza.
- Venetian-related transactions face additional collateral requirements because their risk assessment has been raised.
- Venetian institutions are denied Koini Trapeza **emergency funding**; the lender-of-last-resort function does not protect them during this retaliatory regime.

### No selective default on 황은채

- Roman state debt is **not invalidated merely because a Venetian owns it**.
- Principal and interest are recorded as paid on schedule.
- Instead of being remitted to Venice, the money enters a special Koini Trapeza escrow account.
- The policy therefore preserves the formal credibility of Roman sovereign debt while denying Venice immediate access to the cash.

### Neutral-Venetian exception

- Venetian houses may submit their books to prove non-participation in the financial attack and swear commercial neutrality.
- After review, qualifying houses are excluded from the escrow regime and may continue existing trade.
- They still:
  - lose tax exemptions temporarily,
  - face additional collateral requirements,
  - must use designated trapezai,
  - remain subject to central clearing.
- The policy therefore deliberately distinguishes the Venetian republic/Dandolo-aligned attack from individual Venetian merchants willing to remain commercially neutral.

## Commercial and Adriatic counterpressure

- Alexios orders representatives of **Genoa and Pisa** summoned, explicitly expecting them to seek market share vacated by Venice.
- Envoys are ordered toward:
  - Emeric,
  - Dalmatian cities,
  - Ancona.
- **Skepsis** is specifically ordered to contact elites in **Zadar** rather than treating Zadar only as an open diplomatic target.
- The Roman answer therefore expands from financial defense into deliberate pressure on Venetian commercial competition and Adriatic political vulnerabilities.

## Chapter endpoint

- The chapter ends with the panic stabilizing and Roman policy moving from defense to retaliation:
  - **“이제 베네치아가 피를 흘릴 시간이었다.”**
- Venice's political, financial and military reaction is not yet established.
