# Chapter 122 Canon — 무엇을 남길 것인가

> Source: author-approved manuscript `manuscripts/reviewed/122.무엇을 남길 것인가.txt`.
> Only facts stated on-page are locked. Exact later legal outcomes, battle details, and long-term succession consequences remain open unless specified below.

## Venice — Dandolo consolidates the counter-coup

- Dandolo exploits the absence of a modern standing army/professional police structure and the continued political relevance of armed household/party followings.
- He times the strike for a Ziani-faction gathering and uses:
  - Dandolo armed retainers/party followers,
  - German mercenaries sent by Henry VI.
- Key points around San Marco, bridges, landings, bells/towers, Rialto and the ducal center are seized or controlled to prevent opposition mobilization.
- The following day, Dandolo asks the Minor Council and Great Council to ratify a temporary delegation of special authority for investigating the alleged anti-republic conspiracy and stabilizing the crisis.
- Pro-Dandolo councillors set the tone while armed Dandolo men are visibly present.
- One strongly pro-Ziani Minor Councillor is detained and forced to resign.
- Two other Ziani-aligned councillors are offered pardon if they publicly say Ziani deceived them.
- Two neutral councillors are persuaded on state-security grounds.
- Existing and newly selected pro-Dandolo councillors provide an institutional façade for the delegated authority.
- This authority is presented as council-ratified special power, not as an inherent permanent ducal emergency prerogative.

## Pietro Ziani

- Dandolo explicitly decides not to kill Ziani at this stage because death would create a martyr and strengthen the opposition's republican legitimacy.
- Ziani is confined in a monastery outside Venice while the alleged Eastern Roman collusion is investigated.
- The confinement also functions as political leverage against Ziani's armed following and allies.
- Ziani's final legal status, release, exile, trial or death is not yet established.

## Merchant accommodation and coercive consolidation

- Dandolo promises major Venetian merchants compensation for losses in Eastern Roman trade through Henry VI:
  - favored access to southern Italian ports,
  - customs advantages,
  - commercial privileges in imperial lands,
  - military transport contracts.
- He argues that defeating Eastern Rome will prevent Venice from losing the eastern Mediterranean commercial contest.
- Remaining Ziani armed followers attempt to remove Dandolo by force.
- Dandolo's armed followers use firearms supplied by the German youths sent by Henry VI.
- The gunfire suppresses the open armed challenge around San Marco.
- Open resistance ceases for the moment, but opposition is described as suppressed rather than eliminated.
- Dandolo concludes that his regime can survive only by winning the war against Eastern Rome.

## Henry VI — Venice as an instrument

- Henry receives confirmation that the Venetian coup has succeeded and believes Venice has effectively fallen into his strategic orbit.
- Henry does not intend to share Eastern Roman spoils with Dandolo.
- Through Constance and the Sicilian crown, he intends to assert that he has inherited the Sicilian kingdom's claims concerning Greece and Albania.
- He regards Genoa and Pisa as usable commercial powers whose neutrality in the Two-Romes war would already count as success.
- With Venice secured, he plans greater intervention in Hungary and Serbia.
- His ideological objective remains to defeat Eastern Rome and vindicate the western empire as the sole Rome under a strengthened Hohenstaufen monarchy.

## 1199 shock — Philip II captured at Bouvines

- Imperial chancellor Conrad of Querfurt brings urgent news to Henry VI.
- In **1199**, Philip II of France has been decisively defeated at **Bouvines** by Richard I of England and taken prisoner.
- The chapter does not yet establish:
  - the exact date of the battle,
  - full order of battle,
  - tactical sequence,
  - casualties,
  - Philip's conditions of captivity or release.

## Richard I — altered post-crusade trajectory

- Alexios previously warns Richard to beware an apparently insignificant soldier / "blind crossbow."
- In this timeline Richard is not captured by Henry VI after the crusade.
- Therefore the enormous historical ransom burden and associated cash outflow to Germany do not occur.
- The narrative states that this reduces taxation resistance and popular resentment toward the royal government.
- John's rebellion does not occur in the same way, and Philip II loses the opportunity to exploit Richard's absence to strip Normandy on the historical scale.
- Richard remains warlike and expensive, but his overall fiscal burden is lower than in original history.
- Under the crusade settlement, Richard receives a share of customs revenue from Damietta, Haifa and Acre.

## Richard's military-administrative development

Over several years Richard's interest in gunpowder and firearms produces state-building side effects:
- royal arms workshops,
- powder magazines,
- improved military-accounting practices,
- port military depots,
- attempts to standardize firearm barrels and ammunition,
- royal procurement officials.

The narrative motive is military effectiveness rather than ideological administrative reform.

## Edward — Richard's legitimate son

- Richard takes a more deliberate interest in Berengaria of Navarre for dynastic reasons.
- In the story, Berengaria sincerely loves Richard.
- Their legitimate son **Edward** is born in **1194**.
- Edward is Richard's only son and acknowledged legitimate heir.
- Fatherhood causes Richard to think in terms of what he will leave to his heir, not only what he will personally achieve.

## Succession precautions

- Richard establishes a regency framework in case he dies before Edward comes of age.
- Berengaria and Eleanor of Aquitaine are designated as guardians.
- Hubert Walter and William Marshal are added to the regency council.
- Richard elevates William Marshal to **Earl of Pembroke earlier than in original history** for this political and military role.
- Hubert Walter is Archbishop of Canterbury and Justiciar, charged with coordinating English government.
- John remains a politically ambitious royal uncle and a succession risk to be contained.

## Richard's continental objective

- Richard resolves that he will no longer remain Philip II's subordinate in respect of his continental lands.
- His objective is to leave Edward the lands on both sides of the Channel without the existing French royal overlordship.
- The narrative describes the desired end state as an independent new realm/empire under the Angevin house.
- This does **not yet** establish a formal imperial title, coronation, or constitutional name. Treat it as Richard's political project until a later manuscript specifies otherwise.
