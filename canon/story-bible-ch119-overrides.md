# Story Bible Override — Chapter 119

> Extends the reviewed manuscript endpoint through chapter 119 and supersedes stale endpoint statements in older high-level canon files.

## Current canonical endpoint

- Latest reviewed manuscript: **Chapter 119, `성 마르코 공황 (2)`**.
- The story remains within the immediate aftermath of the 1198-04-25 Saint Mark Panic; chapter 119 itself does not state a new exact calendar date.
- The Two-Romes financial war has moved from market attack into full Roman institutional crisis management.

## Established response

- Roman ministries and financial institutions initially panic because this is their first real large-scale financial panic.
- Existing reforms nevertheless let them rapidly produce:
  - cross-institution transaction records,
  - emergency treasury reports,
  - one-day simplified balance sheets,
  - maturity-structure analysis,
  - solvency/liquidity distinctions.
- Alexios orders preservation of systemic functions rather than blanket rescue of bank owners.

## Chrysocheir resolution — established

- Leon Chrysocheir loses management control.
- His equity absorbs losses first.
- His personal assets are pledged within the scope of guarantee responsibility.
- Viable payment/deposit and sound-asset functions are transferred to a temporary trapeza.
- Impaired/speculative assets and bad guarantees go to a liquidation estate.
- The old failed entity leaves central clearing; the temporary institution replaces it.

## Koini Trapeza — established crisis powers in practice

- Emergency collateralized lending with substantial haircuts.
- Above-normal emergency interest rates.
- Emergency `황은채` collateral treatment.
- Central clearing and daily net settlement.
- Centralized handling of large grammation conversions.
- Preservation of small grammation redemption and selected deposit/payment accounts.

## 황은채

- Treasury mass repurchase is rejected.
- Pre-panic holdings may receive emergency collateral valuation of 70% of face even while market price is near 60%, preventing panic-driven forced sales and arbitrage by post-panic buyers.
- Constantinople's `황은채` decline stops by panic day four and begins to reverse; provincial stabilization is expected later.

## Political institutions

- Synkletos and Syllogos are both mobilized.
- A Syllogos quorum is expected within roughly one week because a regular session was already approaching.
- The exact constitutional quorum percentage is not newly established.
- The crisis requires spending beyond the current budget.
- Alexios sends an emergency supplementary budget to Syllogos despite the manuscript stating that direct imperial authority would be legally sufficient.
- His stated rationale is institutional legitimacy and demonstration of Roman systemic strength.
- Parliamentary approval itself remains unresolved at the chapter endpoint.
