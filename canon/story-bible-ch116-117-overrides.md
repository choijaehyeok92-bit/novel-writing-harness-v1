# Story Bible Overrides — Chapters 116–117

> These later reviewed chapters supersede stale endpoint statements that stop at chapter 115. For unchanged material, earlier story/world canon remains in force.

## Current canonical endpoint

- Latest reviewed manuscript: **Chapter 117, `양 로마 대전 (2)`**.
- Chapter 116 opens the wider Two-Romes proxy war from **1196 onward**.
- Chapter 117 establishes that the initial proxy-war fronts do not collapse into immediate Roman defeat; instead, several fronts harden into stalemate while a new Angevin–French western front opens and Venice prepares a major financial offensive.

## Coalition alignment now established on-page

### Henry VI / anti-Roman network
- Henry VI is the principal organizer connecting multiple anti-Roman actors rather than personally commanding every front.
- Confirmed aligned or supported actors by ch116–117 include:
  - Andrew of Hungary,
  - Vukan in Serbia,
  - Philip II of France,
  - Levon of Cilician Armenia,
  - Qutb al-Din Malikshah and anti-Roman Seljuk amirs,
  - Enrico Dandolo / the Venetian anti-Roman strategy.
- Conrad of Querfurt is established in-scene as Henry's imperial chancellor and bishop of Hildesheim.

### Roman-aligned network
- Alexios supports legitimate or already-subordinate regional rulers rather than replacing them:
  - Emeric in Hungary,
  - Stefan Nemanjić in Serbia,
  - Kaykhusraw in the reduced Seljuk polity,
  - the Lampron Hetumids against Levon.
- Roman support combines limited regular troops, advisers, money, crossbows, limited gunpowder weapons, intelligence, logistics, naval pressure and manipulation of existing local rivalries.

## Strategic doctrine demonstrated

- Henry's concept is multi-front pressure: force Rome to disperse its main field army by simultaneous crises in Hungary, Serbia, Anatolia, Cilicia and western Europe.
- Chapters 116–117 establish the counter-thesis: Rome can absorb pressure through **vassals, allied rulers, local factions, staff-supported limited deployments, logistics, naval interdiction and financial defenses**.
- The initial proxy war therefore demonstrates state-system resilience rather than simple battlefield superiority.
- This strengthens the ongoing thematic line that the coming qualitative Roman advantage is institutional rather than merely technological.

## Current front status after ch117

- **Hungary:** Andrew's early success stalls after an Emeric/Roman counterstroke near Lake Balaton; the Drava crossing zone becomes the main division between Andrew's Croatian-Dalmatian-Slavonian position and Emeric's Hungarian/Pannonian position.
- **Serbia:** Vukan's initial expansion from Zeta stalls after defeat in Raška; the Lim–Ras–Ibar–Zvečan zone becomes the hardened internal front.
- **Anatolia:** Malikshah threatens but cannot take Caesarea and is simultaneously harassed from the rear by a Roman-vassal Danishmendid polity around Niksar–Tokat–Amasya.
- **Cilicia:** Levon is pinned by a Lampron Hetumid revolt and cannot freely exploit the anti-Roman alignment.
- **Adriatic:** Roman and Venetian fleets have confronted one another over rebel supply shipping, but no general naval war has yet occurred.
- **France/Angevin realm:** Richard repudiates his feudal subordination to Philip over his continental fiefs; Philip treats this as rebellion and calls in Henry's promised support.
- **Finance:** Dandolo uses the Adriatic incident to bring more Venetian middle opinion behind confrontation and prepares a renewed attack on Roman banks and credit.
