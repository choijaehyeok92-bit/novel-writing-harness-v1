# Story Bible Override — Chapter 118

> This file extends the reviewed canon endpoint through chapter 118 and supersedes stale endpoint statements in older high-level canon files.

## Current canonical endpoint

- Latest reviewed manuscript: **Chapter 118, `성 마르코 공황`**.
- Exact date: **1198-04-25**.
- The Two-Romes conflict has now escalated from dynastic, military and diplomatic proxy warfare into a coordinated financial attack on the Roman credit system.

## Saint Mark Panic — established

- The financial crisis later receives the historical name **성 마르코의 공황**.
- Venetian merchants trigger the initial shock through coordinated deposit withdrawals and sales of Roman state securities.
- The crisis then becomes self-propagating as Roman depositors react to rumor and falling confidence.
- `Kitrina Fyla` rumor sheets materially amplify the panic.
- Heavy calls on guaranteed commercial bills and refusal of normal extensions turn off-balance-sheet/contingent obligations into immediate claims.
- Forced sales by Leon Chrysocheir, previously one of the major buyers of `황은채`, accelerate the bond collapse.
- `황은채` falls below 60% of face value within several days.
- Collateral deterioration and inability of borrowers to obtain cash create a broader liquidity crisis.
- Leon's trapeza suspends payments while still book-solvent at the moment described in the chapter.

## Leon Chrysocheir — established characterization

- Megas Trapezites, “황금 손,” and the most aggressive large banker in the empire.
- Combines deposits, trade finance, bond dealing, bill guarantees, maritime lending and long-duration commercial investment.
- He is explicitly competent; his failure grows from aggressive maturity/liquidity transformation and confidence that systemic importance plus Koini Trapeza will make rescue unavoidable.
- Theodore Argyros serves as the on-page conservative warning voice against Leon's expansion.

## Dandolo — established financial doctrine

- Dandolo understands both the strengths and the moral-hazard weakness of Koini Trapeza.
- He knowingly chooses a financial attack likely to damage Venice's remaining Roman privileges because previous proxy fronts have not imposed sufficient costs.
- Venice is itself attempting to imitate the Roman central-bank model, but domestic factional interests impede institutional creation.

## Political setting

- Innocent III is established as newly elected in January 1198.
- In this timeline he openly supports Emeric and excommunicates Andrew.
- Henry VI and Dandolo both face meaningful internal political opposition while subsidizing the broader anti-Roman coalition.

## Unresolved at endpoint

- whether grammation convertibility is altered,

The endpoint is Alexios declaring that Koini Trapeza, Synkletos and Syllogos were created for a crisis like this.
