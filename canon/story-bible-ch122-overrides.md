# Story Bible Override — Chapter 122

> Extends the reviewed manuscript endpoint through chapter 122 and supersedes stale endpoint statements in older high-level canon files.

## Current canonical endpoint

- Latest reviewed manuscript: **Chapter 122, `무엇을 남길 것인가`**.
- The Two-Romes conflict has expanded from Roman–Venetian financial confrontation into:
  - Dandolo's authoritarian consolidation in Venice,
  - direct Henry VI involvement in that consolidation,
  - a widening contest for Hungary/Serbia,
  - and a new western disruption caused by Richard I's victory over Philip II.

## Venice

- Dandolo neutralizes the Ziani leadership before it can execute the chapter-121 council strategy.
- Councils survive institutionally but ratify special delegated authority under intimidation.
- Pietro Ziani is confined alive in a monastery.
- Dandolo secures part of the merchant elite by promising western-imperial commercial compensation.
- An armed Ziani reaction is suppressed with firearms.
- Opposition survives underground; Dandolo's regime is not socially secure.
- Dandolo believes victory over Eastern Rome is now necessary for his own political survival.

## Henry VI

- The German coup participants are now confirmed as sent by Henry.
- Henry considers Venice effectively captured into his camp.
- He does not intend to reward Dandolo as an equal partner.
- He plans to use the Sicilian crown's inherited claims, commercial rivalry among the Italian maritime communes, and intervention in Hungary/Serbia against Eastern Rome.
- He seeks a Hohenstaufen political order in which his empire vindicates the claim to be the sole Rome.

## Richard I

- The post-crusade captivity/ransom crisis does not happen.
- The resulting fiscal and political pressures are lower than in original history.
- John's historical rebellion does not develop.
- Philip II gains less opportunity to strip Richard's Norman position during absence.
- Richard invests heavily in gunpowder/firearms, creating secondary improvements in procurement, storage, accounting and military logistics.
- Richard and Berengaria have a legitimate son, **Edward**, in 1194.
- Edward's birth changes Richard's governing horizon from personal achievement toward dynastic legacy.
- Richard establishes a regency/guardianship structure and strengthens Hubert Walter and William Marshal.
- Richard seeks to leave Edward a cross-Channel inheritance freed from French royal overlordship.

## 1199 Bouvines

- Richard defeats Philip II at Bouvines.
- Philip is captured.
- This result is canonical.
- The battle's detailed mechanics and diplomatic settlement remain open.

## Thematic endpoint

Chapter 122 frames three competing ideas of legacy:
- Dandolo: preserve Venetian greatness even by coercing the republic;
- Henry VI: build enduring Hohenstaufen supremacy and monopolize Roman legitimacy;
- Richard I: build a durable inheritance for Edward.

The chapter title's question — what a ruler leaves behind — is therefore a governing theme, not only a Richard-focused beat.
