# World Bible Override — Chapter 121

## Constantinople Venetian bailo — INTENTIONAL ALTERNATE-HISTORY CANON

- In this timeline, a prior Roman-Venetian treaty has already established a **Venetian bailo in Constantinople before 1198**.
- The office is therefore intentional alternate-history canon.
- Reviewers must **not** flag the use of `바일로 / bailo` in chapters 121 and related earlier/later scenes merely because the historical office is more securely attested in later centuries.
- Historical review may compare the office to real Venetian diplomatic/commercial representation, but canon controls the timeline divergence.

## Venice — constitutional operating assumptions now on-page

Chapter 121 establishes the following for the story's Venice in 1198:

- The doge is a lifetime office but resignation is politically possible and has recent precedent.
- The doge cannot freely execute major public policy without council support.
- The **Minor Council is six-member in the story's current constitutional practice**.
- Great Council and Minor Council support materially constrain the doge.
- Dandolo's Saint Mark financial operation was deliberately conducted through aligned merchants/merchant houses rather than openly as a council-approved state attack.
- The Ziani faction treats that circumvention as a violation of Dandolo's sworn limits.
- A council revolt can make the doge politically unable to act even before any formal resignation.

## Dandolo — current political doctrine

- Dandolo's strategic objective is not subordination to Henry VI.
- He views both empires as potential threats to Venetian independence.
- He temporarily prefers the western empire because, in the current timeline, Henry VI lacks an independent maritime system and therefore needs maritime partners.
- Dandolo believes Rome's growing institutional/financial strength will otherwise make Venice permanently subordinate.
- By chapter 121 he increasingly identifies his own strategy with Venice's survival.
- Political opponents can therefore be reclassified in his mind as threats to the republic rather than merely rivals.

## Armed seizure — limits of what is established

- German-accented armed men seize the Ziani opposition meeting.
- Their exact employer/recruitment channel remains **UNRESOLVED**.
- They are not yet established as:
  - official Venetian state troops,
  - official Holy Roman imperial troops,
  - a permanent ducal guard,
  - a formal police force.
- Do not canonize any of those identities until a later manuscript states them.
- The narrative does establish Dandolo as the political author/beneficiary of the action.

## Review guardrail

When reviewing chapter 121 or its aftermath:
- treat the seizure as an intentional **off-book coercive counter-coup** unless later canon changes it;
- do not silently convert the force into a modern police unit or standing national army;
- require later chapters to address how Dandolo attempts to legalize, conceal or propagandize the detention of major Venetian elites.
