# External Actors Update — Chapters 116–117

## Henry VI
- Now functions as the principal coalition-builder of the anti-Roman proxy war.
- Supports Andrew, Vukan, Philip II, Levon and Malikshah through different channels rather than treating them as identical subordinates.
- His strategic aim is to create enough simultaneous pressure that Alexios must disperse Roman power.
- By chapter 117 this strategy has created real crises but has not broken the Roman system.

## Enrico Dandolo
- Expands anti-Roman strategy from trade competition into explicit financial coercion, rebel supply and Adriatic security politics.
- Uses the Roman naval interdiction incident to pull more Venetian middle opinion toward confrontation.
- Ends chapter 117 preparing a larger attack on Roman banks and credit.

## Andrew of Hungary
- Rejects Emeric's succession and seeks the crown.
- Receives German and Venetian aid.
- Wins at Mački and acquires substantial Croatian/Dalmatian/Slavonian power.
- Loses momentum after defeat near Lake Balaton against Emeric with Roman support.
- Remains active and dependent on outside subsidy.

## Emeric of Hungary
- Recognized by the papacy and supported by Alexios as legitimate king.
- Recovers from Mački with Roman support and stabilizes the Hungarian core.

## Vukan
- Becomes rallying point for anti-Roman Serbian nobles.
- Aligns with Andrew and western support rather than with Emeric.
- Expands from Zeta but is defeated at Raška after Roman intervention.
- Retains enough western/coastal support for the conflict to remain unresolved.

## Stefan Nemanjić
- Roman-vassal Grand Župan supported directly by a Roman tagma under Kaloyan.
- Retains the inland Raška core after defeating Vukan's offensive.

## Philip II
- Aligns strategically with Henry despite being Alexios's brother-in-law.
- Sees Richard's strong Angevin realm and Alexios–Richard relationship as threats to Capetian power.
- After Richard repudiates feudal subordination, Philip seeks and receives Henry's promised support.

## Richard I
- Returns from the successful crusade without the historical captivity/ransom episode.
- Opens a new western front by rejecting continued feudal service to Philip for his continental holdings.
- Uses a juristic argument based on crowned/anointed kingship while also accusing Philip of first breaking feudal good faith.

## Qutb al-Din Malikshah
- Survives as an alternate-history anti-Roman Seljuk claimant.
- Re-emerges at Sivas with local amir support.
- Fails to take Caesarea and becomes pinned by both Kaykhusraw's defense and attacks on his rear.

## Kaykhusraw
- Remains Roman-dependent sultan.
- Chooses to hold Caesarea rather than seek a risky open battle.
- Receives Roman supplies, specialists, weapons and a resident tagma.

## Levon
- Joins the anti-Roman alignment seeking greater independence and advantage around Antioch.
- Cannot fully exploit the alliance because the Lampron Hetumids revolt with Roman encouragement.

## Conrad of Querfurt
- Chapter 116 establishes him on-page as Henry's imperial chancellor and bishop of Hildesheim.
- Advises Henry to supplement the independence offer to Levon with an Antioch-related incentive.

## Alexios Gidos
- Commands Roman forces supporting Emeric in Hungary and helps reverse Andrew's momentum near Lake Balaton.

## Kaloyan
- Commands a Roman tagma in direct support of Stefan Nemanjić.
- Helps defeat Vukan's offensive at Raška.

## Konstantinos Angelos Doukas
- Established in chapter 117 as a Roman admiral operating in the Adriatic.
- Attempts interdiction of Venetian rebel-supply shipping but avoids an unnecessary general battle near Venice's favored waters.
