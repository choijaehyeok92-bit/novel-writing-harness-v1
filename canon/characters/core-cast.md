# Core Character Canon

> Current states through chapter 107. Character knowledge is separated from objective canon where it matters.

## Alexios II Komnenos

- Role: protagonist; Roman emperor/autokrator.
- Start: 1180-08-01, child co-emperor/heir at Manuel I's deathbed.
- Identity: modern Korean traveler's memory/personality synchronized with the historical Alexios II's memories and emotions.
- Supernatural assets: perfect recall, extraordinary physical health/recovery/development, high combat/command aptitude; still mortal to lethal trauma.
- Core desires: survive; protect Anna; make Roman revival durable; leave Manuel a state governed by institutions rather than court roulette.
- Temperament: workaholic, intellectually aggressive, enjoys military command, uses jokes/modern metaphors in internal voice, politically theatrical when useful.
- Political style: recruit talent, create incentives/institutions, deliberately pit interests against each other, then use overwhelming imperial authority to lock the resulting rules in place.
- Moral risk: increasingly willing to accept manipulation and mass violence when framed as state survival/integration; knows this about himself and does not claim innocence.
- Current status (1194): dominant emperor, recoverer of Jerusalem, father of co-emperor Manuel, architect of a transformed Roman state; now focused on consolidation/social integration while anticipating rivalry with Venice/Henry's western empire.
- First appearance: ch1.

## Anamnesis (Ana)

- Role: supernatural contract partner and voice in Alexios's consciousness.
- Self-description at start: low-ranking imp/demon; caused the transfer and contract mistake.
- Power state: most power spent on transfer; no normal independent physical embodiment after time resumes.
- Function: dialogue partner, memory/context support, skeptic, comic counterpoint, moral pressure.
- Contract stake: if Alexios gives up/fails the mission, both face soul extinction as stated in ch1.
- Important continuity rule: do not confuse **Ana (아나)** with **Anna (안나)**.
- Hidden identity/original backstory: **UNCONFIRMED IN CURRENT MANUSCRIPT**. Reviewers must not fill it from inference.
- First appearance: ch1.

## Anna / Agnes of France

- Role: Augusta/Basilissa; Alexios's wife; later regent and mother of Manuel.
- Origin: French princess Agnes, adapted/renamed Anna in Roman court.
- Core traits: gentle and emotionally open early, but grows into a disciplined ruler; maintains strong affection for Alexios while learning formal imperial procedure.
- Relationship with Alexios: initially a child marriage, becomes genuine mutual love and trust; she is his main private emotional anchor.
- Political development: by the crusade absence she can present a formal two-year regency report, supervise wartime finance/황은채, and prepare contingency succession measures if Alexios does not return.
- Child: Manuel Porphyrogennetos, born 1191-01 by context.
- Current status: Basilissa and proven regent; partner in securing Manuel's dynasty.
- First appearance/recalled: ch1; active court role from early chapters.

## Manuel Porphyrogennetos

- Role: son of Alexios II and Anna; child co-emperor.
- Birth: 1191-01 by story context; ch92 text `1991년 1월` is an open mechanical typo.
- Status by 1192: elevated under the new succession law; publicly receives legal ratification, patriarchal coronation/rite, military/civic proclamation as required by the new system.
- Narrative function: converts Alexios's reform question from "can I rule?" to "can this system survive me?"
- Current status (1194): young child/co-emperor; no independent political agency yet.

## Manuel I Komnenos

- Role: father of Alexios; dying emperor at story start.
- Character function: high benchmark and shadow over Alexios — genuine great-power ruler whose arrangements still historically failed to secure his son.
- Story divergence contribution: Alexios persuades him to redesign the regency into competing restraints and to preserve surveillance/support channels.
- Status: deceased in 1180.

## Maria of Antioch / Xene

- Role: Alexios's mother; Dowager Empress and early regency center.
- Early political problem: her Latin/Antiochene identity and court allies make her both protective mother and lightning rod for anti-Latin politics.
- Later trajectory: withdraws from active palace politics and takes monastic life under the name **Xene**, focusing on charitable/medical religious work.
- Current status: alive in monastic/charitable sphere as last established.

## John Komnenos Vatatzes

- Role: senior Komnenian commander/statesman; one of Alexios's foundational protectors and mentors.
- Importance: gives military/political weight to the young emperor's survival, later becomes an elder symbol of the first reform generation.
- Alternate-history divergence: survives approximately ten years beyond his historical death date (manuscript explicitly compares to 1182).
- Status: dies around 1192; his death marks generational turnover.

## John Kamateros Doukas

- Role: senior civil administrator; later permanent Megas Logothetes coordinating the reorganized cabinet.
- Function: practical state manager and bridge between old elite competence and new institutional state.
- During crusade: part of the regency support structure around Anna.
- Status by ch95: elderly (70s), retires/resigns from top office together with Kontostephanos, triggering succession among younger administrators.

## Andronikos Kontostephanos

- Role: senior naval/military commander; one of Alexios's original power anchors.
- Major contributions: naval security, Norman war, crusading era command.
- Status by ch95: elderly, retires from top command; later officeholders take over naval high command.

## Alexios Branas

- Role: major field commander; Megas Domestikos in the mature regime.
- Established actions: suppresses Theodore Mangaphas's Philadelphia revolt, defends against Turkic threats, serves throughout reform/war campaigns.
- Character tension: historically capable of rebellion, so Alexios values his talent while keeping institutional/political control over him.
- Current status: active Megas Domestikos; favors hard military response to Turkic unrest in ch105.

## Niketas Choniates

- Role: reform intellectual/administrator; finance and institutional policy specialist.
- Early rise: drawn into Alexios's new advisory/administrative system as young talent.
- Functions across story: tax/accounting reform, fiscal analysis, regency support, policy drafting.
- Current status: senior reform technocrat; part of the younger generation poised to replace retiring first-generation grandees.

## Theodore Balsamon / Patriarch Theodore II

- Role: canon-law expert, diplomat, later Ecumenical Patriarch.
- Political character: conservative Orthodox legalist who can cooperate with Alexios but insists on church/legal boundaries.
- Crusade role: key intermediary with Rome/West and later patriarch during Alexios's absence.
- Current conflict: accepts the legal ingenuity of Alexios's political definition of "Roman" but fears secular integration may weaken the Church's normative role.
- Current status: Patriarch Theodore II; institutional counterweight, not simple antagonist.

## Leon (Technon / imperial workshop engineer)

- Role: chief practical technologist/engineer, later senior figure in Logothesion ton Technon.
- Traits: blunt, craft-focused, less interested in abstract theory than making things work.
- Established work: secret gunpowder/firearms program; printing/mechanical projects and technical diffusion under imperial direction.
- Current status: active senior technical administrator; by the 1190s can distribute non-secret devices such as spinning-wheel designs broadly.

## Nikephoros, Ophthalmarches

- Role: head/senior controller of Ophthalmos security apparatus.
- Function: counter-subversion, sensitive investigations, workshop secrecy, protection, deniable coercive capability.
- Structural development: Alexios later strips audit functions from Ophthalmos because he fears an over-powerful security organ could dominate weaker successors.

## Andronikos Komnenos

- Role: principal early antagonist and ideological mirror.
- Position: historically destined usurper; in this timeline leads armed challenge earlier and is defeated.
- Trial thesis: attacks Latin privilege, Komnenian nepotism, tax abuse, church corruption, and frontier neglect — several criticisms are materially true.
- Defeat: Alexios concedes the problems but argues Andronikos brought a sword rather than accounts/petitions/law, stealing his reformist legitimacy.
- Status: executed in 1181.
- Continuing narrative function: Alexios repeatedly measures his own appetite for power and coercion against the path Andronikos represented.
