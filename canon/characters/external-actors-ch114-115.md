# External Actors Addendum — Chapters 114–115

> This addendum supersedes chapter-113 status lines in `canon/characters/external-actors.md` where later events are now established.

## Henry VI — UPDATED

- Henry's Sicilian campaign succeeds rapidly across the southern mainland with an imperial force described as about **10,000**.
- He reaches the Strait of Messina without needing a decisive field battle, but is stopped strategically by the Roman-backed San Raineri fortress and Roman-Sicilian maritime denial.
- Henry recognizes that he cannot safely land large cavalry, horses, siege equipment and supplies on the island while Roman and Sicilian naval power remains active.
- He attempts to draw on Venice, Genoa and Pisa; Venice is checked by Roman naval power on the Otranto route, while Genoa and Pisa turn back rather than enter an open Roman war.
- Henry accepts the immediate Roman proposal to stop at the mainland and orders preparations for Constance's coronation, but treats this as a temporary strategic defeat rather than settlement of the larger rivalry.
- He explicitly vows to acquire a fleet, seize Constantinople and force Alexios to kneel.
- Current status through ch115: **mainland victor, island denied, openly committed to future naval and imperial confrontation with Alexios**.

## William III / Guglielmo III — UPDATED

- The mainland regime collapses before Henry's advance.
- William survives on the **Sicilian island** because Roman-backed Messina defenses and Roman/Sicilian naval power deny Henry a viable crossing.
- Roman diplomacy explicitly seeks guarantees for William and Sibylla's personal safety.
- Current status: **surviving child king on the island under Roman strategic protection; mainland lost**.

## Sibylla of Acerra — UPDATED

- Remains with the surviving island regime after the mainland collapses.
- Her and William's safety is an explicit condition raised by the Roman envoy in negotiations with Henry.
- Current status: **regent/mother of the surviving island claimant under Roman-backed deterrence**.

## Margaritus of Brindisi / Sicilian naval establishment — UPDATED

- Chapter 114 confirms that the Sicilian navy remains willing to fight for the surviving regime under conditions of Roman backing.
- A Sicilian fleet sorties from Palermo against Genoese/Pisan forces during the maritime crisis.
- This resolves the earlier open question of whether the Sicilian navy would remain aligned: at this stage it does.

## Enrico Dandolo — UPDATED

- The Otranto incident becomes a decisive argument in Dandolo's domestic political case.
- Dandolo now argues openly in the Great Council that Venice's Roman trade boom is creating dangerous strategic dependence.
- His doctrine is not framed as simple hatred of Greeks: he points to Roman finance, standards, market access, fleet power and the memory of 1171 as evidence that Venice must resist before dependence becomes irreversible.
- He explicitly identifies the emerging Roman gold-convertible **grammation** and Roman-required shipping standards as signs of systemic dominance.
- He considers the strategic conflict to have already begun at Otranto.
- Current status: **Doge and leader of a preventive anti-Roman strategic line, but still opposed by a substantial commercial-conciliation faction**.

## Pietro Ziani — NEW CURRENT POLITICAL ACTOR

- Son of former Doge Sebastiano Ziani.
- Presented as a man whose wealth and network made him a plausible top-tier dogal contender in 1192, though he is not described as having literally fought a two-man election against Dandolo.
- In chapter 115 he becomes the principal on-page critic of Dandolo's anti-Roman course.
- Ziani argues that current Roman policy is commercially favorable, that war could destroy Venetian privileges, and that Genoa/Pisa would benefit if Venice abandoned the Roman market.
- He accuses Dandolo of staking Venetian citizens on a dangerous geopolitical gamble.
- Current status: **leading face of the commercial-conciliation / anti-war Venetian bloc**.

## Domenico Sanudo — UPDATED ROLE

- Supports Ziani's effort to clarify to Constantinople that the Otranto confrontation was not intended as a Venetian war against Rome.
- Functions as an experienced eastern-diplomacy voice within the conciliatory camp.

## Pietro Michiel and Ottaviano Quirino — CURRENT ROLE

- Both align with Ziani in chapter 115 against Dandolo's confrontational policy.
- They are part of the on-page Venetian eastern-policy bloc favoring continued commercial access and caution toward direct war.

## Béla III of Hungary — STATUS CHANGE

- Dies in **1196**.
- His death opens the Hungarian succession environment that narration explicitly identifies as a coming arena of eastern-western Roman proxy competition.

## Stefan Nemanja — STATUS CHANGE

- In **1196** abdicates the Serbian grand-princely position in favor of his second son **Stefan Nemanjić** and withdraws to monastic life.
- His abdication opens the Serbian succession environment that narration explicitly identifies as another coming arena of eastern-western Roman proxy competition.

## Stefan Nemanjić — UPDATED

- Receives the Serbian grand-princely position from Stefan Nemanja in 1196.
- Precise later alignment, rivals, western/eastern support and the timing of open conflict are not yet established by chapter 115.
