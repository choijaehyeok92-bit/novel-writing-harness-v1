# World Bible Override — Chapter 118

## Finance — Saint Mark Panic

- By 1198 the Roman private banking sector is mature enough that major trapezai routinely transform deposits into longer-duration commercial and financial assets.
- Leon Chrysocheir demonstrates the most aggressive version of this model.
- Chrysocheir's institution combines deposit banking, trade finance, bond dealing, bill guarantees, maritime lending and commercial investment.
- The system is vulnerable to simultaneous withdrawal demands, forced asset sales and collateral-price feedback.
- `황은채` functions as a major liquid/tradable state security and as collateral within private credit relationships.
- During the Saint Mark Panic, its price falls below 60% of face value.
- `Kitrina Fyla` has enough social reach to amplify financial rumor into market action.
- Grammation is used as an immediately demanded payment instrument alongside gold and silver; chapter 118 does not establish any suspension of its convertibility.

## Koini Trapeza

- Koini Trapeza is sufficiently well known that major bankers expect it to function as a systemic backstop in a panic.
- This expectation has generated an explicit **moral-hazard problem**: Leon believes his systemic importance makes rescue unavoidable.
- Chapter 118 does **not** yet establish the actual rescue terms, lending limits, collateral rules, deposit guarantees, resolution procedure or fiscal backstop used in the panic.

## Venice

- Venice is attempting to imitate the Roman central-banking model, but factional/family interests prevent easy institutional replication.
- Venetian financial warfare is now an explicit state-level strategic instrument in the conflict with Rome.

## Political institutions

- Alexios identifies Koini Trapeza, Synkletos and Syllogos as the institutions intended to handle a crisis of this magnitude.
- The exact division of emergency financial, legislative and fiscal authority among these bodies remains unresolved until a later manuscript enacts it.
