# Story Bible

> Source of truth: manuscript ZIP imported 2026-09-04. This file records only facts established by the manuscript or explicit system rules stated in-scene. Planned-but-not-yet-realized ideas are not treated as established facts.

## Work identity

- Working title: **3년 뒤 살해당하는 동로마 황제가 되었다**
- Genre/tone: alternate history, Byzantine court politics, war, state reform, web novel.
- Narrative core: a modern Korean traveler survives a plane crash only by being sent into **Alexios II Komnenos** in 1180 and must overturn the historical death awaiting him, then turns survival into a project to rebuild the Roman Empire.

## Starting point

- **1180-08-01**: time is temporarily frozen in Manuel I Komnenos's bedchamber at Blachernae.
- The protagonist has been transferred into Alexios II Komnenos after the crash of an Istanbul-to-Incheon flight.
- Historical baseline known to the protagonist: Alexios II would be murdered by Andronikos Komnenos roughly three years later.
- Immediate goals: survive, prevent Andronikos's takeover, protect Anna/Agnes, and obtain room to rule in his own right.

## Supernatural / possession rules

- The protagonist's modern memories and Alexios II's existing memories are fully synchronized; there is no split-personality conflict or language-acclimation problem.
- He possesses perfect autobiographical/learned recall, including both lives, and can retrieve future knowledge deliberately.
- Physical advantages stated by Anamnesis: immunity to ordinary disease, abnormally fast wound recovery, unusually rapid muscular development, very high combat/command aptitude, and a natural-life expectancy of roughly ninety years **unless killed by trauma**.
- Rhetoric/social performance also receives supernatural assistance, but not omniscience: the protagonist can still misjudge people and institutions.
- **Anamnesis (Ana)** spent most of her power on the transfer. After time resumes she normally has no independent physical body and communicates from within Alexios's consciousness.
- Abandoning/failing the contract is stated to result in soul extinction for both Alexios and Ana.
- Ana's deeper identity, original motive, and any reincarnation-level secret beyond what the narrator currently knows are **NOT YET CONFIRMED IN THE MANUSCRIPT** and must not be inferred by reviewers.

## Protagonist doctrine and long-term goals

The protagonist's program expands in stages:

1. **Survival and legitimacy** — prevent Andronikos's historical seizure of power and the anti-Latin catastrophe.
2. **State capacity** — create reliable taxation, records, courts, merit recruitment, communications, education, and a professional bureaucracy.
3. **Military recovery** — defeat Sicily, stabilize Serbia/Balkans, rebuild field and naval power, and exploit gunpowder cautiously.
4. **Anatolian recovery** — break Rum Seljuk power and restore direct Roman rule in central/western Anatolia while retaining a dependent Rum polity farther east.
5. **Western accommodation without submission** — reduce the cycle of Latin-Byzantine hatred that historically enables the Fourth Crusade while defending Roman strategic autonomy.
6. **Institutionalized rule** — distribute parts of imperial authority to courts, the reformed Synkletos, Syllogos, audit organs, and legally defined succession so that the system can outlive one extraordinary emperor.
7. **Political Roman identity** — gradually widen "Roman" beyond Greek/Orthodox ethnicity to subjects who accept Roman law, taxes, protection, and loyalty, while preserving a distinct ecclesiastical category for Orthodox membership.

## Core moral trajectory

- Alexios does not define good rule as personal innocence. He accepts that sovereign decisions can kill people and repeatedly chooses order, victory, and long-term state survival over moral purity.
- He prefers law, incentives, registration, and institutions where they can work, but will use coercion, intelligence operations, confiscation, execution, and war when he judges them necessary.
- By the 1193–1194 nomad-integration arc he openly admits that he engineered pressure on Turkic tribes, destroyed groups that refused the new Roman order, and will bear the resulting guilt if it leaves a stronger state to his son.
- This ruthlessness is **not** to be normalized away by reviewers; it is a deliberate character feature and a continuing source of tension with Ana, the Church, and the institutions Alexios himself creates.

## Core relationships

### Anna / Agnes of France
- Alexios's child bride at the start, renamed Anna in Roman court life.
- The original Alexios's affection for her is inseparable from the protagonist's synchronized memory; protecting her is the first emotional reason he chooses to fight rather than give up.
- Relationship evolves into genuine marriage/love and political partnership.
- During the long crusade she rules as Basilissa/regent and demonstrates independent administrative competence.
- She is a primary emotional restraint on Alexios's instrumental view of power.

### Anamnesis (Ana)
- Contract partner, internal companion, commentator, and conscience/foil.
- Frequently challenges Alexios's self-justifications but also assists him with memory/context.
- Must be distinguished from Basilissa **Anna** in all continuity work.

### Manuel, Porphyrogennetos
- Son of Alexios and Anna; manuscript context establishes birth in **1191-01** (chapter 92 contains an obvious mechanical typo "1991년 1월").
- Elevated as child co-emperor under the new succession system by 1192.
- Alexios increasingly frames institutional reform as a state he can safely hand to Manuel rather than a realm dependent on himself alone.

## Major locked alternate-history divergences through chapter 107

- 1181: Andronikos Komnenos is defeated, publicly tried, and executed; his historical takeover does not occur.
- Therefore the historical 1182 Latin massacre under Andronikos does not occur in the same form.
- Roman state reform begins years earlier and at much greater scale than history.
- The Sicilian/Norman invasion is decisively defeated and becomes a source of imperial legitimacy rather than collapse.
- Serbia is forced back into subordinate status after the Norman war settlement.
- Gunpowder firearms and cannon are developed in state workshops by the late 1180s.
- A reformed Synkletos and representative Syllogos become durable political institutions.
- Frederick Barbarossa survives the Third Crusade route and cooperates with Alexios in a "Two Romes" crusade.
- Rum power is broken; Iconium falls; Kaykhusraw rules a Roman-dependent Rum polity while substantial Anatolian territory returns to direct Roman control.
- Jerusalem is recovered by coalition war and negotiated settlement without a general massacre; Conrad of Montferrat becomes king.
- Antioch and Cilician Armenia are brought into/confirmed within Roman suzerainty on revised terms.
- Alexios returns with immense crusading legitimacy and promulgates a written imperial succession law in 1192.
- Roman political identity is widened to include loyal non-Orthodox subjects, including Muslims, without making them members of the Orthodox Church.
- By 1194 Turkic integration has moved from military settlement to registration, contracts, economic incorporation, and shared imperial popular culture.
