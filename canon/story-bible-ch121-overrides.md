# Story Bible Override — Chapter 121

> Extends the reviewed manuscript endpoint through chapter 121 and supersedes stale endpoint statements in older high-level canon files.

## Current canonical endpoint

- Latest reviewed manuscript: **Chapter 121, `공화국을 위하여`**.
- The immediate Saint Mark Panic itself has stabilized, but the Roman retaliation has now triggered a constitutional crisis inside Venice.
- The Two-Romes conflict has moved from proxy war → financial attack → Roman economic retaliation → Venetian internal coercion.

## Constantinople

- Venice's treaty-established bailo protests the retaliation.
- Roman officials reveal documentary evidence tying Venetian merchant houses to the Saint Mark Day operation:
  - cipher message,
  - decoded text,
  - decryption key/table.
- Rome warns that it does not seek war but will escalate if Venice chooses it.

## Dandolo

- Dandolo recognizes the Roman response as an institutional success.
- He does not retreat.
- He believes delay will make Roman superiority irreversible.
- His partnership with Henry VI remains instrumental because Henry lacks an autonomous maritime system in the current timeline.
- Dandolo's political self-conception hardens into: only he understands what must be done to preserve Venice.

## Venetian opposition

- Pietro Ziani leads a coalition with Domenico Sanudo, Pietro Michiel and Ottaviano Querini.
- They seek Dandolo's resignation through council obstruction and political isolation rather than a modern impeachment mechanism.
- Their plan:
  - control/block the six-member Minor Council,
  - deny Great Council support for further anti-Roman escalation,
  - deny retaliatory war funding,
  - demand negotiations with Rome,
  - then demand Dandolo's resignation.
- They argue that the Saint Mark operation bypassed the Great Council and violated Dandolo's sworn constraints.

## Constitutional rupture

- Before the opposition acts, German-accented armed men seize the Ziani meeting.
- The captors accuse the opposition of Eastern Roman collusion and announce forced "retirement."
- Dandolo is narratively established as responsible for the coercive move.
- The exact legal status, recruitment channel and final disposition of the detainees remain unresolved.
- This is now a central thematic contrast:
  - Alexios institutionalizes restraint during crisis;
  - Dandolo breaks institutional restraint in the name of saving the republic.

## Intentional divergence / review note

- The Constantinopolitan **bailo** is established by an earlier treaty in this alternate timeline.
- Do not flag the office as an anachronism without first checking canon.
