- By the end of chapter 120, the immediate panic has substantially stabilized within roughly one month and the conflict has entered an explicit Roman retaliatory phase.

## Syllogos — updated current state

- Founding/first apportionment: **166 seats**.
- Current chapter-120 size: **216 seats**, after population growth and integration of recovered Bucellarian and Anatolikon territories.
- Emergency attendance: **165 members**.
- Kyanophoroi remain the majority party since 1196 but are underrepresented at the emergency sitting because their support/member base is more rural and provincial.

## Fiscal-constitutional settlement

- Asen/Kyanophoroi support anti-Venetian retaliation while rejecting a blanket public bailout of reckless trapezitai.
- Alexios rejects the proposal to place legislators directly inside treasury execution.
- He instead establishes:
  - pre-finalization Syllogos budget review,
  - a Syllogos recommendation power,
  - continued legal non-binding status of those recommendations on the emperor.
- This is now canon as a constitutional convention/self-binding imperial practice.
- The emergency supplementary-budget support resolution passes unanimously among all 165 present members and exceeds two-thirds of the full 216-seat house.
- The measure therefore goes directly to the emperor and the manuscript treats the supplementary spending as effectively approved.

## Synkletos role

- Synkletos is given a crisis inquiry/hearing role over the Saint Mark Panic and failed trapezai.
- Leon Chrysocheir is publicly questioned.
- The practical institutional division now visible on-page is:
  - emperor/cabinet — execution,
  - Syllogos — fiscal review/legitimacy,
  - Logisterion — professional audit,
  - Synkletos — political inquiry/hearing.

## Saint Mark Panic — immediate outcome

- The panic substantially stabilizes within about one month.
- Roman institutions have preserved the payment system and sovereign credit while avoiding a simple owner bailout.
- Dandolo's attack has imposed real costs but has not forced Roman capitulation.

## Roman retaliation against Venice — enacted

- Venetian receivables are placed into Koini Trapeza escrow pending compensation, without transfer of ownership.
- Venetian commercial privileges are temporarily suspended.
- Venetian houses are forced through designated trapezai and Koini Trapeza clearing, with additional collateral.
- Venetian institutions are excluded from Koini Trapeza emergency funding.
- Venetian-owned `황은채` remains valid and is paid on schedule into escrow rather than selectively defaulted.
- Venetian merchant houses that prove non-participation through books and swear commercial neutrality may be released from escrow and continue trade, though without tax exemptions and under tighter clearing/collateral rules.
- Genoa and Pisa are invited to exploit the commercial opening.
- Rome expands pressure into the Adriatic through Emeric, Dalmatian cities, Ancona and covert Skepsis contact with Zadar elites.
