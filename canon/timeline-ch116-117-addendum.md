# Timeline Addendum — Chapters 116–117

> Additive timeline for later reviewed manuscripts. Where chapter 117 does not restate an exact year, the event is kept relative rather than assigned a date by inference.

| Date / phase | Event | Status | Source | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1196 onward** | Henry VI's anti-Roman network begins coordinated multi-front pressure. Andrew challenges Emeric; Vukan's supporters rise against Stefan Nemanjić; Malikshah mobilizes anti-Roman Seljuk amirs; Levon mobilizes; Venice finances/supplies western proxies and launches market pressure; France prepares | CONFIRMED | 116 | The Two-Romes rivalry becomes an active proxy war across multiple regions |
| 1196 onward | Papacy recognizes/supports Emeric's legitimate Hungarian kingship rather than backing Vukan's anti-Roman project | CONFIRMED | 116 | Papal policy indirectly aligns with the anti-Henry side without creating a full Roman-papal military alliance |
| 1196 onward | Alexios accelerates aid to Emeric and Stefan, strengthens eastern defenses, supports Kaykhusraw, activates Lampron Hetumids, prepares supply networks and Roman naval interdiction of rebel military shipping | CONFIRMED | 116 | Roman response is distributed across local allies, logistics, finance, intelligence and limited military deployments |
| post-opening phase, exact date not restated | Andrew's army is defeated near Lake Balaton by Emeric with Roman troops under Alexios Gidos | CONFIRMED | 117 | Hungarian front hardens around Drava crossings; Andrew retains much of Croatia, Dalmatia and Slavonia while Emeric holds the Hungarian/Pannonian core |
| same phase | Kaloyan leads a Roman tagma to support Stefan Nemanjić; Vukan is heavily defeated at Raška | CONFIRMED | 117 | Serbian front hardens along upper Lim–west of Ras–Ibar–Zvečan |
| same phase | Roman fleet attempts to interdict Venetian military supply shipping toward Croatian/Dalmatian ports; Venetian fleet responds; Roman admiral Konstantinos Angelos Doukas declines general battle and withdraws after the warning action | CONFIRMED | 117 | Roman-Venetian confrontation becomes overt without yet becoming general naval war |
| same phase | Malikshah advances from Sivas but fails to take Caesarea; Roman supplies and a resident Roman tagma strengthen Kaykhusraw's defense | CONFIRMED | 117 | Anatolian proxy front reaches siege stalemate |
| same phase | Roman-vassal Danishmendid polity around Niksar–Tokat–Amasya raids Malikshah's Sivas rear | CONFIRMED | 117 | Malikshah is pinned between Caesarea and rear-area attacks |
| same phase | Lampron Hetumids rebel against Levon | CONFIRMED | 117 | Levon's field strength is tied down in the Taurus and his freedom to move toward Antioch is sharply reduced |
| same phase | Richard I issues Philip II a final ultimatum and renounces feudal service regarding Normandy, Anjou and Aquitaine | CONFIRMED | 117 | Angevin–French war front opens |
| same phase | Philip II invokes prior agreement with Henry VI; Henry promises money, credit, recruiting access, specialists, some troops and limited gunpowder weapons | CONFIRMED | 117 | Western imperial resources begin feeding the French war without Henry personally shifting his main army west |
| ch117 endpoint | Dandolo, strengthened politically by the Adriatic confrontation, prepares a renewed attack on Roman trapezai, credit and finance | CONFIRMED / CURRENT ENDPOINT | 117 | The next phase is set to move from proxy warfare into major financial warfare |
