# World Bible Override — Chapter 123

## Western Europe after the 1199 Bouvines settlement

Chapter 123 supersedes the chapter-122 "OPEN mechanics" status of Bouvines on the following points.

### Richard / Angevin polity

- Richard remains king of England and lord of a cross-Channel dynastic complex.
- His continental lands listed in the treaty — Normandy, Anjou, Maine, Touraine, Poitou, Aquitaine and Gascony — are no longer treated as fiefs under French royal overlordship.
- This does **not** make those lands ordinary English shires/provinces.
- Do not silently flatten the Angevin polity into a unitary centralized English state.
- Their internal laws, institutions and privileges remain subject to later/local canon unless explicitly changed.
- Edward is internationally strengthened as the recognized heir to Richard's cross-Channel inheritance.
- Richard does **not yet** hold a formal imperial title.

### France / Capetians

- Philip II survives Bouvines but is captured and compelled to accept the settlement.
- The Capetian monarchy continues.
- Philip and crown prince Louis recognize Edward's succession rights under the settlement.
- France loses the feudal-superior legal position over the listed Angevin continental lands.
- France is bound to a 15-year peace and to the anti-rebel/non-intervention clauses established in chapter 123.
- A secret clause removes Philip from Henry VI's anti-English / anti-Eastern-Roman military alignment for at least 15 years.

### Early western field artillery

Richard's field-artillery development is an intentional alternate-history branch.

Established characteristics:
- derived from Richard's observation of Roman gunpowder use at Damietta and later royal-workshop imitation;
- lighter/shorter than heavy siege cannon;
- reduced caliber;
- transported by reinforced carts;
- wheels fixed with wedges and the piece supported for firing;
- useful from prepared/preselected positions;
- cannot freely redeploy across a battlefield like mature later field artillery.

Review guardrail:
- do not upgrade this into fast horse artillery or mature standardized early-modern artillery;
- do not infer precise caliber, range, rate of fire, number of guns or carriage geometry unless later manuscript supplies them;
- France also has gunpowder weapons, so Richard's advantage is not a monopoly but operational adaptation.
