# World Bible Override — Chapter 120

## Political system — current Syllogos size and fiscal practice

### Syllogos seat count

- The original/first-apportionment Syllogos remains correctly recorded as **166 seats** in older canon.
- By chapter 120, after population growth and incorporation of recovered **Bucellarian** and **Anatolikon** territories, the current Syllogos has expanded to **216 seats**.
- This is a later reapportionment/expansion and does **not** retroactively change the original 166-seat founding design.
- The chapter 120 emergency sitting has **165 members present**.

### Budget review — ESTABLISHED CONSTITUTIONAL PRACTICE

- Before a budget is finalized, it is to be submitted to Syllogos for review.
- Syllogos may issue recommendations on the budget.
- These recommendations do **not legally bind the emperor**.
- The practice is nevertheless deliberately established by Alexios as a self-binding political/constitutional precedent intended to be difficult for later emperors to discard casually.
- Direct participation by Syllogos members in treasury execution is rejected in order to preserve the boundary between administration/executive and assembly.
- Existing rule preserved: a Syllogos measure supported by two-thirds of the full house goes directly to the emperor rather than being blocked by Synkletos.

### Synkletos financial inquiry

- Synkletos may conduct political inquiry/hearing into the Saint Mark Panic and failed trapezai.
- This is distinct from:
  - Syllogos fiscal review,
  - Logisterion professional audit,
  - executive administration by the emperor/cabinet.
- Chapter 120 establishes this crisis inquiry in practice; it does not by itself define an unlimited modern parliamentary investigation power over every executive subject.

## Venetian economic-war regime — ESTABLISHED AS RETALIATORY/EMERGENCY POLICY

### Escrow

- Venetian receivables inside the empire may be diverted into Koini Trapeza escrow pending compensation for the coordinated financial attack.
- Covered categories established on-page include:
  - proceeds of goods sold,
  - bills owed by Roman merchants,
  - maritime-loan repayments,
  - `황은채` principal and interest.
- Escrow does **not** transfer ownership.
- Before compensation is assessed, neither officials nor the emperor may dispose of the funds arbitrarily.
- This is a retaliatory measure against Venice, not a general rule that all foreign property may be confiscated.

### Venetian privileges

Pending investigation/compensation:
- tax exemptions are suspended,
- use of exclusive Venetian quay/warehouse facilities is suspended,
- financial-attack cases are moved to imperial-court jurisdiction,
- new Venetian merchant-house/agency registration is halted.

### Koini Trapeza treatment of Venetian institutions

- Venetian houses cannot use independent settlement/clearing within the Roman system.
- Payments must pass through designated trapezai and Koini Trapeza processing.
- Additional collateral may be required because Venetian transaction risk is re-rated upward.
- Venetian institutions are excluded from Koini Trapeza emergency funding during the retaliatory regime.
- This does not mean every Venetian commercial transaction is prohibited.

### State debt credibility

- `황은채` held by Venetians is not voided.
- Principal and interest remain due and are booked on schedule.
- Cash that would otherwise be remitted to Venice is instead credited into special escrow.
- Chapter 120 therefore explicitly rejects selective sovereign default as a retaliatory tool.

### Neutral Venetian merchants

- A Venetian merchant house may submit books proving non-participation in the attack and swear commercial neutrality.
- After review, qualifying houses may be released from escrow and continue existing business.
- They remain subject to:
  - suspended tax exemptions,
  - additional collateral,
  - designated-trapeza routing,
  - central clearing.
- The policy deliberately seeks to distinguish neutral Venetian commercial actors from the political/financial attack directed by Dandolo-aligned interests.

## Adriatic counterpressure

- Rome invites Genoa and Pisa to compete for commercial space vacated by Venice.
- Formal outreach is directed toward Emeric, Dalmatian cities and Ancona.
- Skepsis is ordered to make elite contacts in Zadar.
- The policy marks an expansion of the Two-Romes financial war into Adriatic commercial and covert competition.
