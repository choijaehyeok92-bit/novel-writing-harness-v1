# World Bible Overrides — Chapters 114–115

> This file supplements `canon/world-bible.md` and supersedes chapter-113-era uncertainty where chapters 114–115 establish new state systems, strategic geography and financial institutions on-page.

## Messina / San Raineri strategic fortress — ESTABLISHED

- Rome and Tancred had previously prepared a fortified denial complex on the **San Raineri peninsula at Messina**, nicknamed the **`Devil's Horn / 악마의 뿔`**.
- It is a deliberate proto-angular artillery fortification rather than a mature early-modern trace italienne.
- Established components include low thick walls, several angular projecting gun positions, a moat, flanking works, protected powder storage, fixed Pyrobolon batteries, low Cheirobolon firing positions, harbor chains, timber works and floating obstacles.
- Stores support roughly **1,000–1,500 defenders** for prolonged resistance.
- Its purpose is not magical control of the full Strait of Messina. It denies safe large-scale unloading of cavalry, horses, siege equipment and supplies and thereby raises the cost of island invasion beyond what Henry can currently accept.
- By chapter 114 the fort openly flies both Sicilian and Roman symbols, including the alternate-history Roman double-headed eagle.

## Roman-Sicilian maritime deterrence — ESTABLISHED

- Roman and Sicilian sea power is sufficient to deny Henry VI an easy crossing after his mainland victory.
- Venice can be checked on the Otranto route by Roman naval concentration.
- Genoa and Pisa remain sensitive to Roman commercial privileges and refuse to treat limited Sicilian contracts as authorization for direct war against the Roman Empire.
- The Sicilian fleet is active and remains aligned with the William III/Sibylla island regime under Roman backing.
- The lesson locked by chapter 114 is strategic: continental conquest does not automatically produce island conquest without secure naval lift and sea control.

## Sicilian political geography after chapter 114

- Southern mainland territory has fallen under Henry's control.
- The Sicilian island remains under the William III/Sibylla regime with Roman strategic protection.
- Henry orders preparation for Constance's coronation, but chapter 114 does not yet show the coronation itself.
- The arrangement is a de facto temporary division and competing claim, not a settled permanent peace.

## Maternal-health system — ESTABLISHED BY 1195

- The Roman state has an empire-level **imperially certified midwife** system.
- Mandatory hygiene includes hand washing before maternal contact and separation from corpse/suppurating-wound exposure.
- Pregnancy risk records include prior births, obstructed labor, severe hemorrhage, miscarriage, suspected twins, fetal movement, persistent bleeding, severe edema, severe headache, visual disturbance, fever and late fetal position.
- Legal/administrative referral pathways allow higher-risk mothers to be moved to specialist hospitals on trained midwife judgment.
- **Bimanual uterine compression** is part of postpartum hemorrhage response.
- **Embryotomy** has a defined extreme-use criterion: obstructed labor with a dying mother and an already-dead fetus.
- These measures are not merely palace practice; chapter 115 explicitly presents them as national/state reforms.

## Grammation — ESTABLISHED FINANCIAL INSTRUMENT

- **`Grammation / γραμμάτιον`** is the in-world term used for Rome's **paper gold-convertible monetary certificate/currency**.
- By 1195 it circulates widely enough for Dandolo to treat its spread as a major Venetian strategic concern.
- It is **not yet canonically the uncontested Mediterranean reserve/standard currency**. Dandolo warns that it is gradually moving toward that position if Venice does nothing.
- The Roman commercial system also imposes standardized shipping-box/container specifications on merchants operating in imperial trade.
- These standards, together with low kommerkion, Roman market scale and naval protection, are now part of the strategic competition with Venice.

## Venice — political economy status by 1195

- Venice is in a strong commercial boom from Roman and Levantine trade.
- Anti-Roman policy therefore does not arise from immediate economic collapse. It arises from a dispute over whether profitable dependence will eventually destroy Venetian autonomy.
- Dandolo leads the preventive-resistance interpretation; Pietro Ziani and allied eastern-policy figures defend de-escalation and continued market access.
- The Venetian political split should remain a coalition of families, investors, diplomats and strategic interests unless a later chapter formalizes modern-style parties.

## Balkan succession transition — 1196

- Béla III's death and Stefan Nemanja's abdication are established date anchors in 1196.
- The manuscript explicitly foreshadows Hungarian and Serbian succession disputes as the next eastern-versus-western Roman proxy arena.
- Do not treat the exact proxy-war camps, intervention mechanisms or battle outcomes as already established until later chapters enact them.
