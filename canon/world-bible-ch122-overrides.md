# World Bible Override — Chapter 122

## Venice — authoritarian settlement after the chapter-121 counter-coup

Chapter 122 supersedes the unresolved chapter-121 aftermath on the following points:

- Henry VI is now confirmed to have sent the German youths/armed personnel used in Dandolo's coup.
- Those Germans provide firearms to Dandolo's armed following.
- Dandolo's men use the firearms to suppress an armed Ziani counter-mobilization.
- Pietro Ziani is not killed; he is confined in a monastery outside Venice while the alleged collusion case remains pending.
- The Minor Council and Great Council continue to exist formally.
- Dandolo obtains council ratification for **temporarily delegated special authority** under coercive conditions.
- Do not reinterpret this as a historically established permanent Venetian "emergency dictatorship" office.
- Dandolo's new order should be reviewed as institutional form preserved under coercive domination.

## Venice — military/public-order framing

The chapter explicitly frames late-twelfth-century Venice as lacking modern standing-army/professional-police institutions and as still permitting political relevance for armed household/party followings.

Historical reviewers should:
- use the existing history note on Venice c.1198 as the external baseline;
- distinguish the story's specific armed followings from a modern private army;
- not silently invent a later professional police magistracy;
- treat the German group as Henry-connected but still of unresolved formal unit/status.

## Henry VI

- Henry sees Dandolo's regime as a means to place Venice in a pro-western-imperial alignment.
- Dandolo's dependence is not reciprocal equality in Henry's mind.
- Henry claims continuity between Constance's Sicilian rights and the Sicilian kingdom's historical claims toward Greece/Albania.
- Genoa and Pisa remain separate actors Henry is willing to bargain with rather than enemies that must automatically be destroyed.

## Richard I — major alternate-history locks

- Richard survives into 1199.
- He is not captured by Henry VI after the crusade.
- The historical ransom crisis is absent.
- John's historical rebellion does not occur on the same path.
- Richard and Berengaria have a legitimate son, **Edward**, born in **1194**.
- Edward is the acknowledged heir.
- Richard creates a regency structure around Berengaria, Eleanor of Aquitaine, Hubert Walter and William Marshal.
- William Marshal becomes Earl of Pembroke earlier than in original history by Richard's decision.
- Hubert Walter functions as Archbishop of Canterbury and Justiciar with responsibility for English government.
- Richard pursues firearms/gunpowder procurement that creates secondary administrative and logistical institutions.
- Richard's long-term continental objective is to remove French royal overlordship and leave an intact cross-Channel inheritance to Edward.
