# Story Bible Overrides — Chapters 114–115

> This file supersedes stale chapter-113 endpoint statements in `canon/story-bible.md`. The source of truth remains the author-approved reviewed manuscripts. Unless contradicted below, the existing story bible remains in force.

## Major locked divergences added by chapter 114

- Henry VI's Sicilian invasion **succeeds rapidly on the southern Italian mainland** but is stopped at the Strait of Messina rather than completing the conquest of the island.
- Tancred had previously concentrated reduced-reparation fiscal room and covert Roman assistance into a fortified Messina/San Raineri denial complex, the **`Devil's Horn / 악마의 뿔`**.
- The fortress is designed to deny secure heavy landing/logistics rather than to dominate the entire strait by artillery.
- Roman and Sicilian naval power, together with the fortress, prevents Henry from moving his army safely onto the island.
- Venice is checked by the Roman fleet on the route toward Otranto; Genoa and Pisa decline to turn the Sicilian contract into an open war with Rome and turn back while a Sicilian fleet moves against them.
- Henry recognizes that he currently lacks the independent naval capability needed to conquer the Sicilian island.
- A Roman-Hohenstaufen confrontation over inherited Sicilian treaty rights ends in a **de facto stop at the strait**. Henry accepts the immediate limit and orders preparations for Constance's coronation, while William III and Sibylla remain on the island under Roman protection.
- The chapter does not yet depict Constance's coronation itself.
- Henry explicitly vows to obtain a fleet, take Constantinople and force Alexios to kneel. Alexios answers the challenge directly. The Sicilian crisis is now an explicit prelude to the broader Two-Romes war.

## Imperial family and maternal-health canon added by chapter 115

- **1195:** Anna safely gives birth to twins in the purple birth chamber: a son **Ioannes / 요안니스** and a daughter **Maria / 마리아**.
- Both children are explicitly born in the purple; the son is hailed as Porphyrogennetos and the daughter as Porphyrogeniti.
- Ioannes is named after Alexios's grandfather; Maria is named after Alexios's mother.
- Manuel is present for the birth sequence and meets the twins.
- By 1195 Alexios has enacted empire-level maternal-health reforms: mandatory hand washing for midwives, separation from corpse/suppurating-wound contact, imperial certification of midwives, structured pregnancy risk records and hospital referral criteria, bimanual uterine compression for postpartum hemorrhage, and defined embryotomy criteria when the mother is dying from obstructed labor and the fetus is already dead.

## Venetian strategic split added by chapter 115

- Venice is simultaneously enjoying an exceptional trade boom under Roman low-kommerkion policy and becoming politically alarmed about dependence on Roman markets, standards, finance and naval power.
- Pietro Ziani becomes the leading on-page advocate of resolving tensions with Rome; Domenico Sanudo, Pietro Michiel and Ottaviano Quirino support the conciliatory/commercial argument.
- Enrico Dandolo develops the opposing strategic doctrine: current prosperity itself is creating future dependence, and Venice must resist before Roman economic and naval dominance becomes irreversible.
- Dandolo explicitly uses the memory of 1171 to argue that dependence on an emperor's policy is inherently dangerous.
- Rome's paper gold-convertible **`grammation / γραμμάτιον`** is established as an emerging commercial instrument. It is **not yet** the uncontested Mediterranean standard; Dandolo warns that it may become one.
- Venetian merchants already use Roman-required standardized shipping boxes/containers in imperial trade.
- The Otranto confrontation becomes a domestic Venetian political fault line. Ziani warns that war risks losing Roman privileges to Genoa/Pisa; Dandolo argues the strategic conflict has already begun.
- Narration describes Venice as politically splitting into two camps around this question.

## Balkan transition added by chapter 115

- **1196:** Béla III of Hungary dies.
- **1196:** Stefan Nemanja abdicates the Serbian grand-princely position to Stefan Nemanjić and enters monastic life.
- The narrator explicitly foreshadows that the Hungarian and Serbian successions will soon become proxy battlegrounds between the eastern and western Roman empires.
- Exact proxy-war alignments, interventions, battle dates and outcomes are **not yet established** by chapter 115.
